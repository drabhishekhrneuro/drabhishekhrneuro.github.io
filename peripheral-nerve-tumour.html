Peripheral Nerve Tumour

What Is a Peripheral Nerve Tumour?

A peripheral nerve tumour is a growth that develops on or inside the nerves that connect your brain and spinal cord to the rest of your body—such as your arms, legs and torso.

The vast majority of these tumours are benign (non-cancerous) and grow slowly, but because they form near delicate electrical wires, even a harmless bump can press on nerves and cause pain or weakness.

Think of a peripheral nerve like a rubber-coated cable containing individual copper wires:

  • The Protective Sheath: Just as a cable has insulation around it, nerves have protective coating cells (like Schwann cells) wrapped around the nerve fibers.
  • The Growth: A nerve tumour occurs when these protective cells grow out of control, creating a small knot or lump along the "cable."
  • The Pinch: As the lump grows larger inside tight tissues or under the skin, it presses against the delicate inner wires, disrupting the signals flowing between your body and your brain.

Common Types

Peripheral nerve tumours are categorized based on how and where they grow relative to the nerve fibers:

  • Schwannomas: The most common type. These grow on the outside surface of the nerve insulation, making them easier to remove without damaging the nerve itself.
  • Neurofibromas: These grow inside the nerve fibers, weaving right through the wires, which makes them a bit more complex to treat.
  • Malignant Peripheral Nerve Sheath Tumours (MPNST): Rare cancerous tumours that grow rapidly and require immediate, aggressive treatment.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms depend on the location and size of the tumour, as well as how much pressure it exerts on the surrounding nerve fibers:

A Painless Bump

Feeling a firm, smooth lump under your skin on your arm, leg or neck that moves slightly from side to side when touched.

The "Electric Shock" Test

Tapping or pressing lightly on the bump triggers a sharp, electric shock or zing shooting down into your fingers or toes.

Pins and Needles

A constant, fuzzy numbness or tingling sensation in the area powered by that nerve—similar to when your leg "falls asleep."

Localized Muscle Weakness

Noticing subtle clumsiness, such as a weaker grip or a leg feeling unusually heavy while climbing stairs.

Common Causes & Triggers

While most peripheral nerve tumours occur sporadically, certain genetic factors can increase the likelihood of developing them:

  • Spontaneous Growth: Most isolated tumours happen randomly with no clear cause or lifestyle trigger.
  • Genetic Conditions: Some people inherit genetic conditions, like Neurofibromatosis (NF1 or NF2) or Schwannomatosis, which make their bodies much more prone to developing multiple nerve tumours throughout life.

How Is It Treated & Managed?

Treatment ranges from periodic observational monitoring with neuroimaging to microscopic surgical removal:

  • "Watchful Waiting": If the tumour is small, benign and not causing pain or muscle weakness, doctors often recommend regular MRI scans to monitor its size rather than operating right away.
  • Targeted Pain Management: Medications specifically designed for nerve pain to calm down irritated signals.
  • Surgical Indications: Surgery is evaluated if the tumour grows rapidly, causes severe pain, creates progressive muscle weakness, or if a biopsy shows signs of malignancy.
  • Microsurgical Resection: Using specialized microscopes, a neurosurgeon carefully peels the tumour away from the healthy nerve fibers. The goal is to completely remove the lump while preserving as many "working wires" as possible to maintain arm or leg function.
