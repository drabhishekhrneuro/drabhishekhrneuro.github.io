Microsurgical Craniotomy and Aneurysm Clipping

What Is Microsurgical Aneurysm Clipping?

Imagine a weakened spot on a delicate blood vessel deep within the brain that balloons outward under arterial pressure, forming a thin-walled sac known as a brain aneurysm. If this wall becomes too thin, it risks rupturing, causing a life-threatening hemorrhage. When an aneurysm cannot be treated from inside the vessel via endovascular coiling, open microsurgical clipping provides a definitive, permanent cure.

Microsurgical aneurysm clipping is an advanced neurosurgical procedure where a specialized keyhole craniotomy is performed to reach the base of the aneurysm, allowing the neurosurgeon to place a tiny, permanent titanium clip across its neck, permanently sealing it off from the body's blood circulation while preserving normal blood flow through the parent artery.

Think of this procedure as placing a precision micro-clamp to permanently neutralize a weak spot:

  • Permanent Anatomical Exclusion: A tiny, spring-loaded titanium clip is snapped across the base (neck) of the aneurysm, stopping blood flow into the sac instantly and eliminating the risk of future rupture.
  • Direct Microsurgical Control: Working under high-magnification operating microscopes, the neurosurgeon meticulously dissects delicate blood vessels and cranial nerves away from the aneurysm dome.
  • Intraoperative Verification: Modern clipping procedures utilize advanced technologies like intraoperative indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence video angiography or micro-Doppler probes to confirm that the aneurysm is completely closed and that normal branch vessels remain fully open.

When Is Aneurysm Clipping Recommended?

Clipping is recommended for unruptured aneurysms at high risk of bleeding or for ruptured aneurysms where microsurgical management offers superior long-term durability:

Ruptured Brain Aneurysms (Subarachnoid Hemorrhage)

Emergency surgical clipping when an aneurysm has burst, sealing the leak to prevent re-bleeding and allowing safe washing out of blood from the basal cisterns.

Complex or Wide-Necked Aneurysms

Aneurysms featuring a wide base or branching blood vessels arising directly from the sac, making them difficult or unsuitable for endovascular coils or flow diverters.

Aneurysms Causing Mass Effect

Large or giant unruptured aneurysms pressing against adjacent cranial nerves—such as the nerves controlling eye movement (causing double vision) or vision.

Younger Patients Seeking Permanent Cure

When long-term durability is a priority, microsurgical clipping provides one of the lowest known rates of aneurysm recurrence over a patient's lifetime.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Meticulous preoperative imaging and vascular mapping are critical to understanding the 3D geometry of the aneurysm and its surrounding branches:

  • Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) & CTA: High-resolution catheter angiography and 3D CT angiography mapping the exact size, neck width, and directional orientation of the aneurysm sac relative to major cerebral arteries.
  • High-Resolution Brain MRI: Assessing brain tissue status, checking for minor ischemic changes, and planning the precise craniotomy corridor (such as pterional, supraorbital, or interhemispheric approaches).
  • Medical & Cardiovascular Optimization: Comprehensive cardiac and anesthetic screening to ensure optimal blood pressure regulation and systemic stability.
  • Multidisciplinary Vascular Planning: Collaborative evaluation by neurosurgeons and neuro-interventionalists to choose the safest, most effective treatment modality for the individual patient.

What Happens During the Surgery?

The procedure is performed under general anesthesia utilizing strict microsurgical standards and neuro-monitoring:

  • Stage 1: Precision Keyhole Craniotomy: A discreet scalp incision is made behind the hairline, and a small, customized bone flap (such as a pterional or keyhole supraorbital craniotomy) is opened at the base of the skull.
  • Stage 2: Cisternal Dissection & CSF Release: The operating microscope is brought into position. Cerebrospinal fluid is gently released from basal cisterns to naturally relax and cushion the brain, minimizing the need for physical brain retraction.
  • Stage 3: Vascular Dissection & Neck Isolation: The parent artery is identified and followed meticulously to the aneurysm. Surrounding delicate perforating vessels and nerves are gently dissected away from the aneurysm dome.
  • Stage 4: Clip Application: Once the neck of the aneurysm is fully isolated, a precisely selected titanium aneurysm clip is applied across the neck. The aneurysm is then gently poked to verify that it is soft, deflated, and completely occluded.
  • Stage 5: Verification & Closure: ICG fluorescent dye angiography or micro-Doppler testing confirms normal blood flow in all parent vessels. The bone flap is secured with titanium plates, and the scalp is closed.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following aneurysm clipping focuses on intensive neuro-vascular monitoring, blood pressure management, and gradual healing:

  • Neuro-ICU Care (First 24–48 Hours): Close monitoring in a specialized neurosurgical intensive care unit for neurological alertness, blood pressure control, and prevention of cerebral vasospasm (especially in ruptured cases).
  • Post-Operative Angiography or CT Angiography: A confirmatory angiogram or CT scan is typically performed before hospital discharge to verify that the clip is perfectly positioned and the aneurysm is fully excluded.
  • Incision Care & Mobilization: Scalp sutures or staples are removed around 10 to 14 days post-op. Early, assisted walking is encouraged to promote circulation and strength.
  • Long-Term Follow-Up: Periodic clinical evaluations and non-invasive vascular imaging ensure long-term stability and a healthy, durable recovery.
