Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)

What Is Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)?

Imagine your brain cradled inside a fluid-filled cushioning system where clear cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is continually produced deep within internal chambers (ventricles) and slowly absorbed into your bloodstream. Under healthy conditions, the rate of fluid production perfectly balances fluid absorption, keeping pressure steady.

Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH) is a specialized brain condition—most common in older adults—where fluid gradually accumulates and expands the internal ventricles without causing a dramatic, sustained spike in overall pressure inside the skull.

Think of your brain's fluid space as a stretched reservoir under subtle structural tension:

  • Gradual Fluid Accumulation: Over time, subtle impairment in fluid reabsorption allows extra fluid to collect, causing the brain's internal fluid chambers to slowly expand.
  • Stretching Critical Nerve Pathways: As the ventricles enlarge, they stretch and press against delicate motor and cognitive nerve tracts running through the surrounding brain tissue—specifically those controlling walking mechanics, bladder function, and short-term memory.
  • Reversible Neurological Condition: NPH is one of the few causes of progressive walking difficulty and cognitive decline that can often be reversed or significantly improved with proper neurosurgical evaluation and treatment.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

NPH is classically recognized by a triad of symptoms, with gait instability typically appearing first:

"Magnetic" Gait Disturbance (Walking Difficulty)

The hallmark symptom: a slow, wide-based, shuffling walk where feet feel "glued to the floor." Patients often struggle with turning, balance, and lifting their feet over small obstacles.

Cognitive Slowing & Memory Decline

Subtle short-term memory loss, difficulty multi-tasking, reduced attention span, apathy, and slower mental processing, often mistaken for normal aging or early dementia.

Urinary Urgency & Incontinence

A sudden, frequent urge to urinate, difficulty holding urine, or eventual loss of bladder control as stretching impacts urinary control pathways in the brain.

How is it Discovered?

Diagnosing NPH requires differentiating ventricular enlargement from general brain atrophy and testing whether fluid drainage directly improves walking:

  • High-Resolution Brain MRI or CT Scan: Imaging shows disproportionately enlarged ventricles out of proportion to general brain tissue shrinkage (Evans Index > 0.3), along with narrowing of upper brain surface folds.
  • Diagnostic High-Volume Lumbar Puncture (Tap Test): A specialized clinical test where a precise volume of spinal fluid is temporarily drained via a lower back tap. Objective walking and cognitive assessments are conducted before and immediately after to check for clear temporary improvement.
  • Extended Lumbar Drainage (ELD): Temporary placement of a fine spinal catheter for 2 to 3 days in a hospital setting to continuously monitor response in complex cases.

How is it Treated?

For patients who demonstrate improvement during diagnostic fluid drainage, surgical diversion offers a definitive path toward restoring mobility and independence:

  • Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunt Placement: The standard neurosurgical treatment. A thin, flexible tube equipped with a pressure-regulating valve is implanted beneath the skin to continuously direct excess CSF from the brain's ventricles into the abdominal cavity, where it is harmlessly absorbed.
  • Programmable Valve Customization: Modern shunts feature externally magnetic, programmable valves that allow your neurosurgeon to adjust fluid drainage settings in the clinic without additional surgery.
  • Lumboperitoneal (LP) Shunt Placement: An alternative diversion method that drains fluid directly from the lumbar spine into the abdominal cavity in appropriate candidates.
  • Post-Operative Gait Rehabilitation: Tailored physical therapy and balance exercises to retrain walking confidence, stride length, and core strength after fluid pressure on motor nerves is relieved.
