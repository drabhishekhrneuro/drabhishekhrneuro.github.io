Endovascular Aneurysm Coiling & Embolization

What Is Endovascular Aneurysm Coiling & Embolization?

Imagine a brain aneurysm as a tiny, fragile ballooning weak spot on an artery inside your head. Under the constant pounding of blood flow, this ballooning sac risks expanding or bursting, leading to a life-threatening brain hemorrhage. Rather than opening the skull bone to clip the aneurysm from the outside, modern neuro-interventions allow specialists to treat the aneurysm directly from within the circulatory system.

Endovascular aneurysm treatment is a minimally invasive, image-guided procedure performed through the body's blood vessels. Using ultra-thin, flexible microcatheters navigated from an artery in the wrist or groin, the endovascular neurosurgeon packs the inside of the aneurysm sac with microscopic platinum coils, stent devices, or flow diverters to block blood flow into the sac completely.

Think of endovascular treatment as an internal repair kit navigated through your arterial highways:

  • Incisionless & Keyhole Access: Access is gained via a pinprick puncture in the femoral (groin) or radial (wrist) artery, completely avoiding scalp incisions or skull opening (craniotomy).
  • Internal Occlusion (Coiling): Ultra-fine platinum coils are released inside the aneurysm sac, inducing controlled blood clotting (thrombosis) that seals off the weak spot while keeping the main artery wide open.
  • Advanced Flow Diversion: Specialized mesh stents (flow diverters) can be placed across the neck of wide-necked or complex aneurysms, redirecting blood flow past the weak area so the aneurysm shrinks and heals over time.

When Is Endovascular Treatment Recommended?

Endovascular coiling or flow diversion is indicated for both unruptured and ruptured brain aneurysms based on individual anatomical and clinical factors:

Ruptured Brain Aneurysms (Subarachnoid Hemorrhage)

Urgent, emergency coiling to rapidly seal a burst aneurysm sac and prevent high-risk re-bleeding without the surgical stress of an open craniotomy.

Unruptured Aneurysms at High Bleeding Risk

Preventative treatment for growing or irregularly shaped aneurysms located in high-risk cerebral arteries to permanently eliminate rupture risk.

Deep or Posterior Circulation Aneurysms

Aneurysms situated deep at the base of the brain or back of the head (basilar tip or vertebral arteries) where minimally invasive intravascular access is significantly safer than open brain surgery.

Older Adults or Medically Fragile Candidates

Providing a safer, gentler cure for patients whose age or medical comorbidities make traditional open surgery higher risk.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Precise high-definition vascular imaging is essential to map the exact 3D architecture of the aneurysm before intervention:

  • Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA): The gold-standard catheter scan providing real-time, 3D rotational views of the aneurysm size, neck width, and relation to branching blood vessels.
  • Multidisciplinary Vascular Consensus: Collaborative evaluation by endovascular neurosurgeons to choose between simple coiling, balloon-assisted coiling, stent-assisted coiling, or flow diverter placement.
  • Antiplatelet Medication Protocol: Pre-procedure blood-thinning medications (such as aspirin or clopidogrel) are administered when stents or flow diverters are planned to prevent blood clots on the implants.
  • Renal & Allergy Screening: Pre-procedure blood tests to verify kidney function and check for contrast dye allergies.

What Happens During the Procedure?

The procedure is carried out in a specialized Hybrid Angiography Suite under general anesthesia with real-time X-ray guidance:

  • Stage 1: Vascular Access & Sheath Placement: Under general anesthesia, a tiny sheath catheter is placed into the radial (wrist) or femoral (groin) artery.
  • Stage 2: Microcatheter Navigation: Guided by live 3D fluoroscopy (roadmapping), an ultra-fine microcatheter is carefully navigated up through the aorta and carotid/vertebral arteries directly into the aneurysm sac.
  • Stage 3: Platinum Coil / Stent Deployment: Microscopic soft platinum coils are introduced one by one into the sac. The coils conform to the shape of the aneurysm, filling it completely. If the neck is wide, a micro-stent or balloon is deployed to hold the coils securely inside.
  • Stage 4: Angiographic Verification: Contrast injections confirm complete occlusion of the aneurysm sac with 100% preserved normal blood flow through the main parent artery.
  • Stage 5: Closure: All microcatheters are removed, and a specialized vascular closure device is applied to the wrist or groin puncture site to seal the artery instantaneously.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following endovascular aneurysm coiling is streamlined due to the minimally invasive, incisionless approach:

  • Short ICU / Ward Stay (24–48 Hours): Unruptured aneurysm patients are typically monitored overnight and discharged home the next day. (Ruptured cases remain in the ICU for specialized subarachnoid hemorrhage care).
  • Puncture Site Care: Bed rest is maintained for a few hours after femoral access. Wrist access allows immediate walking. The tiny puncture site heals within a few days without stitches.
  • Medication Compliance: Strict continuation of prescribed antiplatelet medications if a stent or flow-diverter device was implanted to ensure long-term vessel patency.
  • Follow-Up Angiography (MRA / CTA / DSA): Non-invasive follow-up imaging is scheduled at 6 and 12 months to confirm permanent occlusion and long-term stability of the coils.
