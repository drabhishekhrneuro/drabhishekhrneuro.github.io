Microvascular Decompression (MVD)

What Is Microvascular Decompression (MVD)?

Imagine your cranial nerves as delicate, high-voltage electrical cables emerging directly from your brainstem to relay sensations and movement to your face. Normally, these nerves are completely free. However, as blood vessels age, they can elongate, loop, and press directly against the root entry zone of a cranial nerve, wearing away its protective insulation (myelin).

Microvascular Decompression (MVD) is a specialized, targeted functional neurosurgical procedure designed to relieve this abnormal vascular compression, curing severe facial pain or twitching at the source without destroying the nerve itself.

Think of MVD as separating a pulsing blood vessel away from a sensitive electrical nerve:

  • Targeted Keyhole Access: Through a discreet coin-sized opening behind the ear (retromastoid craniectomy), the neurosurgeon reaches the cerebellopontine angle where cranial nerves meet the brainstem.
  • Isolating the Conflict: Working under high-power operating microscope magnification, the surgeon meticulously identifies the specific offending artery or vein resting on the nerve.
  • Teflon Cushioning: A small, inert sponge (made of medical-grade Teflon) is carefully placed between the pulsating blood vessel and the nerve, acting as a permanent physical cushion that stops the compression and allows the nerve to heal.

When Is MVD Recommended?

MVD is recommended for patients suffering from debilitating cranial nerve compression syndromes that fail to respond adequately to medications:

Trigeminal Neuralgia

Excruciating, shock-like, electric-knife facial pain triggered by light touch, chewing, or brushing teeth, caused by a looping blood vessel compressing the fifth cranial nerve (trigeminal nerve).

Hemifacial Spasm

Involuntary, progressive twitching of the muscles on one side of the face, triggered by a pulsating blood vessel compressing the seventh cranial nerve (facial nerve) as it exits the brainstem.

Medication-Refractory Symptoms

When high-dose anticonvulsants, nerve pain medications, or Botox injections lose effectiveness or produce intolerable side effects.

Medically Fit Candidates Seeking Long-Term Relief

Patients who want a definitive, durable anatomical cure rather than temporary nerve ablation or destructive radiation treatments.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Advanced neuroimaging is crucial to visualizing the exact conflict between the blood vessel and the cranial nerve before surgery:

  • High-Resolution FIESTA / CISS 3D Brain MRI: Specialized thin-slice MRI sequences designed specifically to visualize fluid spaces, cranial nerves, and looping arteries without needing contrast dye.
  • MR Angiography (MRA): Vascular imaging mapping the exact course of arteries and veins surrounding the brainstem and skull base.
  • Comprehensive Neurological Assessment: Evaluating baseline facial sensation, motor function, hearing, and overall medical fitness for general anesthesia.
  • Patient Education & Counseling: Detailed discussion regarding surgical expectations, alternative therapies, and recovery milestones.

What Happens During the Surgery?

The procedure is executed under general anesthesia utilizing advanced intraoperative monitoring and microsurgical technique:

  • Stage 1: Positioning & Retromastoid Keyhole Access: The patient is positioned securely on their side. A small, discreet incision is made behind the ear, and a coin-sized opening (craniectomy) is created in the skull bone.
  • Stage 2: Cerebrospinal Fluid Release & Cisternal Entry: The operating microscope is brought into position. Cerebrospinal fluid is gently released to naturally relax the cerebellum, exposing the cerebellopontine angle without requiring brain retraction.
  • Stage 3: Nerve Identification & Vascular Dissection: Working under high magnification, the neurosurgeon identifies the affected cranial nerve and traces it back to the brainstem to locate the exact artery or vein causing compression.
  • Stage 4: Teflon Cushion Placement: The offending vessel is gently mobilized away from the nerve, and small, specialized Teflon pledgets (pads) are inserted between them, completely insulating the nerve from any further vascular pulsation.
  • Stage 5: Verification & Closure: Intraoperative nerve monitoring verifies nerve integrity. The bone opening is repaired, and the scalp is meticulously closed with cosmetic sutures.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following MVD focuses on incisional healing, gentle mobilization, and monitoring as the irritated nerve settles:

  • Neuro-ICU / Ward Monitoring (First 24 Hours): Patients are monitored closely for neurological stability, balance, hearing preservation, and comfortable pain management.
  • Post-Op Sensations & Healing: Mild imbalance, localized incision soreness, or temporary stiffness behind the ear are common and improve steadily over the first couple of weeks.
  • Pain or Spasm Resolution: For trigeminal neuralgia, pain relief is often immediate upon waking. For hemifacial spasm, muscle twitches may fade gradually over weeks or months as the nerve heals from chronic compression.
  • Activity Restrictions: Strenuous lifting, heavy straining, and vigorous head movements are restricted for several weeks until incisional healing is complete.
