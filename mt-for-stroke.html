Endovascular Thrombectomy (EVT) for Acute Ischemic Stroke

What Is an Endovascular Thrombectomy?

Imagine a major blood vessel supplying your brain as a vital water pipeline. During an acute ischemic stroke, a large blood clot (thrombus) lodges inside one of these primary cerebral arteries, instantly cutting off critical blood flow and oxygen to millions of brain cells every minute. Without rapid restoration of blood flow, the affected brain tissue faces permanent damage.

Endovascular Thrombectomy (EVT)—also known as Mechanical Thrombectomy—is a emergency, minimally invasive neuro-interventional procedure designed to physically retrieve and remove the blocking blood clot from inside the artery, rapidly restoring blood flow to vulnerable brain tissue.

Think of this procedure as an emergency unclogging operation navigated directly inside your brain's arterial network:

  • Direct Clot Retrieval: Using specialized micro-devices—such as stent retrievers (microscopic wire cages) or high-power aspiration catheters—the neuro-interventionalist captures and pulls the clot completely out of the body.
  • Emergency Time-Critical Access: Performed rapidly through a pinprick puncture in the wrist or groin, eliminating the need for open brain surgery while saving millions of brain cells ("time is brain").
  • Immediate Blood Flow Restoration: Reopening the blocked artery (recanalization) provides the highest proven success rate in reversing severe stroke deficits and preventing permanent disability.

When Is Endovascular Thrombectomy Recommended?

EVT is indicated for acute ischemic stroke caused by a Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO) in the brain's primary blood supply lines:

Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO) Confirmed on CTA/MRA

Emergency imaging proving a sudden blockage in major cerebral arteries, including the internal carotid artery (ICA), middle cerebral artery (MCA), or basilar artery.

Acute Severe Stroke Symptoms (BE-FAST Signs)

Sudden onset of facial drooping, arm or leg weakness/paralysis on one side of the body, severe speech difficulty (aphasia or dysarthria), or sudden loss of balance/consciousness.

Favorable Treatment Time Window

Most effective when performed within 6 hours of symptom onset, but can be extended up to 24 hours in selected patients demonstrating salvageable brain tissue on advanced perfusion scans.

Salvageable Brain Tissue (Penumbra Present)

Imaging confirming that while a core region of brain tissue has been affected, a substantial surrounding zone of brain tissue remains viable if blood flow is restored quickly.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Emergency hyper-acute stroke protocols focus on rapid diagnosis and immediate transfer to the catheterization lab:

  • Emergency Non-Contrast Brain CT Scan: Performed immediately upon arrival to rule out intracranial hemorrhage and calculate the ASPECTS score (evaluating early ischemic changes).
  • CT Angiography (CTA) & CT Perfusion (CTP): Multi-phase vascular imaging mapping the exact site of blood clot occlusion, collateral blood flow, and the volume of salvageable brain tissue.
  • IV Thrombolysis ("Clot-Busting" Medication): Intravenous clot-dissolving medication (such as tPA or Tenecteplase) is initiated immediately if the patient arrives within the eligible window, without delaying transfer to the cath lab.
  • Rapid Angio-Suite Activation: The multidisciplinary stroke team (neuro-interventionalist, stroke neurologist, anesthesiologist, and specialized nurses) prepares the hybrid cath lab for immediate arterial access.

What Happens During the Procedure?

The procedure is executed under conscious sedation or general anesthesia in a specialized Hybrid Neuro-Angiography Suite:

  • Stage 1: Emergency Vascular Access: Under local anesthesia and sedation, a guide catheter sheath is introduced into the femoral (groin) or radial (wrist) artery under ultrasound guidance.
  • Stage 2: Microcatheter Navigation: Guided by live fluoroscopy, microcatheters are navigated through the aorta and neck arteries up into the brain, reaching the exact face of the blocking blood clot.
  • Stage 3: Clot Engagement & Retrieval:
    • Stent Retriever Technique: A microscopic nitinol mesh stent is expanded inside the clot, trapping the clot within its wire lattice before being pulled back into the outer catheter.
    • Direct Aspiration Technique: A large-bore flexible catheter is placed directly against the clot, applying high continuous suction to vacuum the clot out of the artery.
  • Stage 4: Flow Restoration Verification: Immediate contrast injections confirm complete reopening of the blood vessel (TICI 2b/3 revascularization) and full restoration of blood flow to the downstream brain tissue.
  • Stage 5: Closure: All catheters are removed, and a specialized arterial closure device is applied to the wrist or groin access site to seal the puncture instantaneously.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Post-thrombectomy care focuses on intensive neurological monitoring, blood pressure optimization, and early rehabilitation:

  • Neuro-ICU Critical Care (First 24–48 Hours): Continuous monitoring in a specialized neuro-ICU with strict blood pressure regulation to optimize brain perfusion and prevent reperfusion injury.
  • Follow-Up Neuroimaging: A repeat brain CT or MRI scan is performed within 24 hours to assess brain tissue recovery, verify clot clearance, and rule out bleeding complications.
  • Stroke Etiology Investigation: Diagnostic workup (echocardiogram, continuous cardiac monitoring, vascular imaging) to identify the root cause of the clot (such as atrial fibrillation or carotid plaque) and prevent future events.
  • Comprehensive Early Rehabilitation: Physical, occupational, and speech therapy programs are initiated immediately in the ICU/ward to maximize functional recovery and help patients regain independence.
