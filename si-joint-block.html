Sacroiliac Joint Injection (SI Joint Block)

What Is a Sacroiliac Joint Injection?

Imagine your pelvis as a rigid protective ring at the base of your spine. The sacroiliac (SI) joints sit on either side of your lower spine, connecting the triangular bone at the base of the column (sacrum) to the large pelvic hip bones (ilium). Reinforced by some of the strongest ligaments in the human body, these joints act as heavy-duty shock absorbers, transferring weight and impact forces between your upper body and lower limbs.

When an SI joint becomes inflamed due to wear-and-tear arthritis, ligament strain, pregnancy-related joint laxity, prior spinal fusion, or traumatic impact, it leads to severe sacroiliac joint dysfunction. This condition causes localized lower back, hip, and buttock pain that frequently mimics lower back spinal pathology or sciatica.

A Sacroiliac Joint Injection (SI Joint Block) is a precise, minimally invasive outpatient procedure. Guided by real-time fluoroscopic X-ray navigation, the specialist delivers a therapeutic mixture of short-acting local anesthetic and concentrated anti-inflammatory corticosteroid directly into the painful SI joint capsule.

Think of an SI joint block as applying targeted anti-inflammatory soothing directly into a stressed pelvic hinge:

  • Dual Diagnostic & Therapeutic Purpose: Immediate pain relief from the local anesthetic confirms that the SI joint is the true culprit causing your symptoms (diagnostic), while the long-acting steroid reduces joint lining inflammation to yield long-term pain relief (therapeutic).
  • Precision Image Guidance: Continuous fluoroscopic X-ray navigation ensures exact needle placement within the narrow, wavy SI joint channel while avoiding nearby surrounding blood vessels and nerves.
  • Unlocks Physical Rehabilitation: Substantially lowering joint inflammation provides a therapeutic window for patients to comfortably participate in targeted physical therapy to stabilize pelvic musculature.

When Is an SI Joint Block Recommended?

An SI joint block is indicated when lower back, buttock, or hip pain stemming from sacroiliac joint inflammation fails to improve with physical therapy or oral medications:

Sacroiliitis & Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction

Localized aching or sharp pain directly over the upper buttock, sacrum, and lower hip region caused by joint arthritis or ligament inflammation.

Pain Aggravated by Weight-Bearing or Transitioning

Pain that predictably worsens when standing up from a seated position, walking up stairs, standing for extended periods, or sleeping on the affected side.

Referred Non-Radicular Leg Pain

Dull aching pain that radiates down into the buttock, groin, or back of the upper thigh, often mimicking sciatica without actual spinal nerve root compression.

Post-Lumbar Fusion Stress Syndrome

SI joint hypermobility and inflammation resulting from increased biomechanical load transferred down after a lower lumbar spine fusion surgery.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Comprehensive clinical assessment and target mapping precede the injection procedure:

  • Targeted Physical Examination: Performing specialized provocative pelvic maneuvers (such as the FABER test, Gaenslen test, and compression tests) to reproduce SI joint pain.
  • Spine & Pelvic Imaging Review: Evaluating X-rays, CT scans, or MRIs to visualize SI joint space narrowing, bone spurs, sclerosis, or joint edema while ruling out primary disc herniations.
  • Coagulation & Blood Thinner Review: Checking blood thinner usage (antiplatelets or anticoagulants) and adjusting them under medical guidance prior to the procedure to minimize bleeding risks.
  • Sterile Pre-Procedure Comfort Preparation: Performed under sterile conditions with local skin numbing; light IV relaxation can be provided for anxious patients.

What Happens During the Procedure?

The injection takes approximately 15 to 20 minutes in a specialized fluoroscopy suite:

  • Stage 1: Positioning & Sterile Field Preparation: The patient lies face-down (prone) on an X-ray transparent table. The skin over the lower back and pelvic region is thoroughly sanitized with antiseptic solution.
  • Stage 2: Local Anesthetic Infiltration: A fine needle numbs the skin and deeper subcutaneous tissues over the lower portion of the SI joint.
  • Stage 3: Image-Guided Needle Advancement: Guided by live fluoroscopic X-ray navigation, a thin spinal needle is precisely steered into the inferior extension of the sacroiliac joint space.
  • Stage 4: Contrast Dye Verification: A small amount of radiopaque contrast dye is injected to confirm under live imaging that the fluid spreads smoothly inside the joint capsule without leaking into adjacent vessels.
  • Stage 5: Therapeutic Injection & Bandage Application: The mixture of local anesthetic and long-acting corticosteroid is slowly injected into the joint. The needle is withdrawn, and a small sterile bandage is placed over the puncture site.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery is rapid, allowing a quick return home and rapid resumption of physical rehabilitation:

  • Immediate Recovery Monitoring (20 to 30 Minutes): Patients are monitored in the recovery area while completing a post-procedure pain assessment to record immediate relief from the local anesthetic.
  • Same-Day Discharge & Daily Activity: Patients return home the same day. Driving is typically restricted for the rest of the day, but normal daily activity can be resumed the following morning.
  • Therapeutic Onset Window: While the local anesthetic provides immediate short-term relief for several hours, the anti-inflammatory corticosteroid takes 2 to 5 days to begin calming joint lining inflammation and providing sustained pain reduction.
  • Active Rehabilitation Facilitation: Pain relief from the block creates an ideal window for physical therapy, focusing on pelvic stabilization, core strengthening, and hip mobility exercises.
