Carotid Artery Stenting (CAS)

What Is Carotid Artery Stenting?

Imagine your carotid arteries as the twin primary conduits delivering oxygen-rich blood from your heart straight to your brain. When fatty cholesterol deposits (atherosclerotic plaque) accumulate along these arterial walls, the vessel narrows (carotid artery stenosis), restricting vital blood flow and creating a constant risk of plaque rupture or blood clot embolization that can cause an ischemic stroke.

Carotid Artery Stenting (CAS) is a modern, minimally invasive endovascular procedure performed entirely from within the blood vessels. Guided by real-time X-ray imaging, an endovascular neurosurgeon navigates a thin catheter from an artery in the wrist or groin up to the neck, deploys a specialized brain-protection filter, inflates a tiny balloon to expand the narrowed channel, and implants a permanent self-expanding metal mesh tube (stent) to keep the artery wide open.

Think of this procedure as placing a high-precision internal scaffold inside your brain's main supply line:

  • Minimally Invasive Access: The entire repair is accomplished through a tiny pinprick access site in the radial (wrist) or femoral (groin) artery, eliminating neck incisions, nerve stretching, or surgical scarring.
  • Embolic Protection Shield: Before opening the blockage, a temporary microscopic mesh filter (embolic protection device) is deployed downstream in the artery to catch any tiny plaque debris that might break loose, protecting the brain throughout the procedure.
  • Durable Arterial Re-expansion: A flexible, self-expanding nitinol stent is deployed across the narrowed segment, pressing the plaque against the arterial wall and permanently holding the blood vessel wide open to restore optimal cerebral blood flow.

When Is Carotid Artery Stenting Recommended?

Carotid stenting is indicated based on the severity of arterial narrowing, stroke symptom history, and patient-specific anatomical factors:

Symptomatic Carotid Stenosis (≥50% to 99% Narrowing)

Recommended for patients who have recently experienced a transient ischemic attack (TIA) or minor stroke caused by plaque shedding from a narrowed carotid artery.

Asymptomatic High-Grade Stenosis (≥70% to 99% Narrowing)

Indicated for individuals with severe carotid narrowing without prior symptoms, to proactively prevent a future catastrophic stroke.

High Surgical Risk for Open Surgery (CEA)

An ideal alternative for patients with significant medical comorbidities (such as severe heart disease), previous neck radiation, prior neck surgery, recurrent stenosis, or high neck artery lesions that are surgically difficult to reach.

Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA) Warnings

Experiencing temporary warning signs including sudden, brief numbness or weakness on one side of the body, slurred speech, or temporary curtain-like vision loss in one eye (amaurosis fugax).

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Comprehensive preoperative vascular imaging and strict antiplatelet preparation ensure maximum procedural safety:

  • High-Resolution Carotid Duplex Ultrasound & CTA: Diagnostic scans to measure exact peak systolic blood flow velocities, quantify stenosis percentage, and evaluate plaque structure (soft vs. calcified).
  • Diagnostic Cerebral Angiography (DSA) / MRA: Comprehensive vascular mapping of the aortic arch, neck arteries, and intracranial brain circulation to plan catheter sizing and access trajectories.
  • Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) Protocol: Patients are started on dual blood-thinning medications (such as aspirin and clopidogrel) several days prior to surgery to prevent blood clots from forming on the catheter equipment or stent mesh.
  • Renal & Cardiac Assessment: Screening kidney function to ensure safe contrast dye clearance and optimizing cardiac parameters prior to endovascular intervention.

What Happens During the Procedure?

The procedure is executed under local anesthesia with light sedation in a specialized Hybrid Angiography Suite:

  • Stage 1: Vascular Access & Sheath Placement: Under local anesthesia, a small sheath catheter is introduced into the radial (wrist) or femoral (groin) artery.
  • Stage 2: Guidance & Embolic Protection Filter Deployment: Guided by live 3D fluoroscopy, a catheter is navigated into the common carotid artery. A fine wire with an expandable basket filter (embolic protection device) is passed beyond the plaque and opened to catch floating debris.
  • Stage 3: Balloon Angioplasty (Pre-Dilation): A tiny, uninflated balloon catheter is positioned across the narrowed plaque segment and gently inflated for a few seconds to stretch open the blocked vessel.
  • Stage 4: Stent Deployment & Post-Dilation: A self-expanding nitinol metal mesh stent is precisely deployed over the plaque, bridging the narrowed segment. A second balloon may be briefly inflated inside the stent to ensure complete expansion against the vessel wall.
  • Stage 5: Filter Retrieval & Puncture Site Closure: The embolic filter (containing captured debris) is collapsed and removed. Confirmatory angiograms verify restored blood flow, all catheters are withdrawn, and a specialized closure device seals the wrist or groin access site.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following carotid stenting focuses on blood pressure stability, dual antiplatelet management, and routine vascular follow-up:

  • Post-Op ICU / HDU Monitoring (First 24 Hours): Close monitoring in a specialized unit for continuous blood pressure management and frequent neurological assessments.
  • Rapid Hospital Discharge: Most patients are able to walk within a few hours (especially with wrist access) and are typically discharged home within 1 to 2 days.
  • Puncture Site Care: Wrist or groin access sites heal rapidly over a few days without stitches. Heavy lifting and strenuous activity are restricted for one week.
  • Dual Antiplatelet Compliance & Follow-Up Scans: Strict adherence to prescribed dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin plus clopidogrel) for a designated period, followed by regular carotid duplex ultrasound scans at 1, 6, and 12 months to verify long-term stent stability and vessel openness.
