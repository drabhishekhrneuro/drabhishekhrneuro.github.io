Posterior Cervical Lateral Mass Fusion

What Is Posterior Cervical Lateral Mass Fusion?

Imagine your neck (cervical spine) as a series of stacked vertebrae supported on either side by thick, sturdy columns of bone known as the lateral masses. When multi-level wear and tear, trauma, severe arthritis, or extensive posterior decompression (such as a multi-level laminectomy) weakens these structural supports, the neck can become unstable, painful, or develop a abnormal forward curve (kyphosis) that puts dangerous pressure on the spinal cord.

Posterior Cervical Lateral Mass Fusion is a specialized surgical technique used to rigidly stabilize and fuse the middle and lower cervical spine from the back of the neck. The surgeon places low-profile titanium screws directly into the dense lateral mass bone on both sides of the vertebrae, connects them with strong titanium rods, and places bone graft to permanently join the stabilized segments into a single solid structure.

Think of lateral mass fusion as building internal structural scaffolding along the back of your neck:

  • Immediate Structural Rigidity: Anchoring screws securely into the thick lateral mass bone provides instant multi-planar mechanical stability, preventing abnormal movement while bone fusion takes place.
  • Decompression Safeguard: Frequently performed alongside a posterior decompression (laminectomy) to ensure that removing the bony roof of the spinal canal does not cause the neck to slip or bend out of alignment over time.
  • High Safety Profile: Screws are angled safely outward and upward (divergent trajectory), steering clear of the sensitive spinal cord running down the center and the vertebral arteries supplying blood to the back of the brain.

When Is Lateral Mass Fusion Recommended?

Lateral mass fixation and fusion are indicated when structural cervical instability or deformity compromises spinal alignment or neurological safety:

Cervical Instability Following Multi-Level Laminectomy

Preventing post-laminectomy kyphosis (swan-neck deformity) when widespread bony decompression removes natural posterior support structures across multiple levels.

Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy with Deformity

Multi-level spinal cord compression accompanied by loss of natural neck curvature, requiring both posterior cord decompression and structural re-alignment.

Cervical Fractures, Dislocations, or Trauma

Reconstructing and stabilizing bone fractures or torn ligaments in the neck caused by severe traumatic injuries.

Adjacent Segment Degeneration or Failed Previous Surgery

Re-stabilizing the cervical spine following progressive breakdown above or below a previous anterior fusion (ACDF), or correcting pseudoarthrosis (unhealed bone fusion).

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Detailed preoperative imaging and trajectory planning are essential for accurate screw placement and patient safety:

  • Fine-Cut Cervical Spine CT Scan (with 3D Reconstruction): The key bony imaging study mapped to evaluate lateral mass size, height, and bone quality to determine precise screw length and trajectory angle (such as Magerl or An technique).
  • CT Angiography (CTA) or MR Angiography (MRA): Mapping the exact anatomical path of the paired vertebral arteries traveling through the transverse foramina adjacent to the lateral masses.
  • High-Resolution Cervical MRI: Visualizing the severity of spinal cord compression, cord signal abnormalities (myelomalacia), and soft tissue ligament integrity.
  • Intraoperative Neuromonitoring (IONM) Setup: Preparing real-time nerve and spinal cord signals (SSEP and MEP) to continuously monitor neurological safety throughout positioning and hardware placement.

What Happens During the Surgery?

The operation is performed under general anesthesia through careful, microsurgical posterior stages:

  • Stage 1: Positioning & Incision: The patient is positioned comfortably face-down (prone) with the head held securely in a specialized padded frame. A clean midline incision is made over the back of the neck, and muscles are gently reflected to expose the lateral masses.
  • Stage 2: Fluoroscopic / Navigation Alignment: Real-time 3D X-ray (fluoroscopy) or optical navigation confirms surgical levels and precise anatomical landmarks on each lateral mass.
  • Stage 3: Lateral Mass Screw Trajectory & Placement: Using microscopic guidance, entry points are drilled on the lateral mass. Pilot holes are angled laterally (outward) and cephalad (upward) to safely avoid the vertebral artery and spinal nerves, followed by placement of polyaxial titanium screws.
  • Stage 4: Decompression (If Indicated) & Rod Contouring: If laminectomy or foraminotomy is needed, decompression is completed. Custom-contoured titanium rods are placed into the screw heads and locked tightly with set screws to maintain ideal neck alignment.
  • Stage 5: Bone Grafting & Multi-Layer Closure: The outer bone surfaces are decorticated (roughened) and packed with autologous or synthetic bone graft to stimulate biological fusion. A temporary surgical drain is placed, and muscles and scalp layers are closed meticulously.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery focuses on protecting the surgical construct, promoting solid bone healing, and restoring functional mobility:

  • Post-Op ICU / Ward Care (1 to 3 Days): Pain management, wound monitoring, and early physical therapy walking begin on the first post-operative day. Surgical drains are typically removed within 24–48 hours.
  • Cervical Brace (Collar) Usage: A rigid or semi-rigid neck collar is typically worn for 4 to 8 weeks following surgery to support the neck muscles and protect the instrumentation while early bone fusion begins.
  • Wound Healing & Activity Restrictions: The posterior neck incision is kept clean and dry until sutures or staples are removed at 10 to 14 days. Strenuous lifting, vigorous bending, and driving are restricted for several weeks.
  • Long-Term Fusion Assessment: Routine follow-up X-rays or CT scans at 3, 6, and 12 months track solid biological bone bridge formation and verify long-term alignment stability.
