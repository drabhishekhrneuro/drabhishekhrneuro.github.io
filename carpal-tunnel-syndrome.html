Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

What Is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome?

Carpal tunnel syndrome is a common condition that causes numbness, tingling and pain in your hand and wrist.

Think of your wrist as having a narrow, rigid tunnel on the palm side:

  • The Tunnel Structure: Inside this tunnel pass important tendons (which curl your fingers) and one major "power line" called the median nerve.
  • The Pressure Build-Up: If the tendons become swollen or irritated from overuse or fluid retention, the tunnel becomes overcrowded.
  • Pinching the Wire: Because the tunnel walls are solid bone and tough tissue, there is no room to expand. The median nerve gets squeezed, causing fuzzy, painful signals to shoot into your thumb and fingers.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms focus primarily on sensory shifts and weakness in the thumb side of the hand:

Hand "Falling Asleep" at Night

Waking up in the middle of the night needing to shake out your hand to "wake it up" because sleeping with bent wrists squeezed the nerve.

Tingling in Specific Fingers

A "pins-and-needles" sensation that affects your thumb, index, middle and ring finger—but notably leaves your pinky finger completely fine (since the pinky relies on a different nerve).

Butterfingers / Clumsiness

Dropping small objects, like difficulty buttoning a shirt, holding a coffee mug or gripping a steering wheel while driving.

Electric Zaps

Occasional sharp, burning shocks shooting from your wrist into your palm and fingers.

Common Triggers

Carpal tunnel syndrome is frequently brought on by repetitive strain, fluid shifts, or underlying health conditions:

  • Repetitive Hand Movements: Hours spent typing, using a mouse, assembly line work or playing musical instruments.
  • Vibrating Tools: Using power drills or jackhammers that send continuous vibration into the hands.
  • Fluid Retention (Pregnancy): Extra fluid build-up during pregnancy swells the tissues inside the tunnel.
  • Underlying Conditions: Arthritis, diabetes or thyroid issues that cause tissue swelling or nerve sensitivity.

How Is It Treated & Managed?

Treatment ranges from simple non-surgical hand care to quick outpatient surgical procedures:

  • Night Splints: Wearing a firm brace at night to keep the wrist straight (neutral position), taking pressure off the nerve while you sleep.
  • Ergonomic Adjustments: Taking regular stretch breaks, lowering keyboard height or switching to an ergonomic mouse.
  • Steroid Injections: Doctors can inject a small dose of anti-inflammatory medicine directly into the wrist tunnel to calm swelling for several months.
  • Surgical Indications: Surgery is evaluated if non-surgical options stop helping, if pain constantly interrupts sleep, or if there is severe, persistent numbness and muscle weakness in the base of the thumb.
  • Carpal Tunnel Release Surgery: The surgeon makes a tiny snip in the tough roof ligament over the carpal tunnel. This "opens up the ceiling," instantly making more room in the tunnel to relieve pressure on the median nerve.
