Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM)

What Is an Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM)?

Imagine your body’s blood circulation as a high-pressure highway system (arteries) connected to a low-pressure local street network (veins), with a series of gentle roundabouts (capillaries) in between to slow down blood flow and deliver oxygen to the brain tissue.

A brain Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM) happens when an abnormal, tangled web of blood vessels directly connects arteries to veins, completely skipping the protective capillary network.

Because there are no capillaries to slow down the blood flow, high-pressure arterial blood rushes directly into veins that are not built to handle such force. Over time, this constant high-pressure flow causes the vessel walls to expand, weaken and strain surrounding brain tissue—carrying a risk of rupture, bleeding or reduced oxygen supply to nearby brain regions.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Brain AVMs may remain hidden until they rupture, but they can also cause progressive symptoms as the tangle grows or diverts normal blood flow away from the brain:

Seizures

Unprovoked seizures (either localized muscle twitches or full-body convulsions) caused by abnormal electrical activity in the brain tissue surrounding the AVM.

Progressive Headaches

Persistent, localized headaches that do not easily respond to standard pain medication, often felt on one specific side of the head.

Unexplained Neurological Weakness or Numbness

Gradual loss of strength, tingling or paralysis on one side of the body, depending on which area of the brain is being affected.

Language, Speech, or Vision Changes

Difficulty speaking, understanding words or sudden partial loss of vision caused by blood flow changes near functional brain centers.

Sudden Severe Bleeding (Rupture)

A sudden, intense "thunderclap" headache accompanied by nausea, vomiting, neck stiffness or loss of consciousness requiring immediate emergency care.

How is it Discovered?

Early and detailed imaging allows your neurosurgeon to map out the precise architecture, blood sources and drainage routes of the vessel tangle:

  • Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) & MRA: Highly detailed structural imaging that identifies the exact location of the vessel tangle, assesses surrounding brain tissue health and maps blood flow patterns.
  • CT Scan & CT Angiography (CTA): A rapid imaging scan used to quickly detect any fresh bleeding in the brain and provide 3D views of the blood vessels feeding the AVM.
  • Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA): The essential gold-standard diagnostic test where contrast dye is injected to capture real-time, high-speed X-ray movies of blood flowing into and out of the AVM, identifying high-risk weak spots or aneurysms within the tangle.

How is it Treated?

Treatment aims to eliminate or isolate the vessel tangle to prevent bleeding, tailored carefully based on the size, location and vascular anatomy of the AVM:

  • Microsurgical Resection: A direct neurosurgical procedure where the surgeon opens a small window in the skull, carefully isolates the feeding arteries and draining veins and completely removes the tangled AVM network from healthy brain tissue.
  • Endovascular Embolization: A minimally invasive procedure where a fine catheter is navigated inside the blood vessels directly into the AVM to inject a specialized "glue" or fluid agent that blocks off high-pressure feeding blood vessels.
  • Stereotactic Radiosurgery (Gamma Knife / CyberKnife): A non-invasive, highly targeted radiation treatment for small or deep-seated AVMs that uses focused beams of radiation to gradually shrink and close off the abnormal vessels over 2 to 3 years.
  • Multimodal / Hybrid Approach: Combining two or more treatments—such as embolization to reduce blood flow followed by surgical removal or radiosurgery—to treat complex or large AVMs with maximum safety.
