Lumbar Disc Prolapse (Herniated Disc)

What Is a Lumbar Disc Prolapse?

Imagine your spine as a stack of supportive bones (vertebrae) that house and protect your spinal cord and nerves. Between each pair of vertebrae lies an intervertebral disc—a rubbery cushion with a tough, fibrous outer ring (annulus fibrosus) and a soft, gel-like center (nucleus pulposus)—acting as a natural shock absorber for daily movements.

A lumbar disc prolapse (commonly referred to as a herniated, slipped, or ruptured disc) happens when the gel-like cushion in your lower back breaks through a tear or weak spot in its outer ring.

Think of your lower back as a busy junction where spinal nerves exit toward your legs:

  • Mechanical Cushion Rupture: Due to wear-and-tear, heavy lifting, or sudden twisting, the gel center pushes outward into the spinal channel.
  • Nerve Root Irritation & Compression: The bulged disc material physically presses on nearby spinal nerve roots exiting the lower back (such as the sciatic nerve).
  • Chemical Inflammation: In addition to physical pressure, the inner gel releases natural inflammatory compounds that irritate nerve fibers, triggering sharp, radiating pain down the leg.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms of a prolapsed lumbar disc vary depending on which nerve root is compressed and how far the disc material has displaced:

Sciatica (Radiating Leg Pain)

A sharp, burning, or electric shock-like pain that starts in the lower back or buttock and travels down the back or side of the thigh, calf, and into the foot.

Localized Lower Back Pain

A dull, aching pain in the lower back that often worsens when sitting, bending forward, coughing, or sneezing.

Numbness & Tingling

"Pins and needles" sensation or loss of feeling in specific regions of the leg, ankle, or toes corresponding to the pinched nerve.

Muscle Weakness ("Foot Drop")

Difficulty lifting the front part of the foot while walking, heaviness in the leg, or sudden knee/ankle weakness.

Cauda Equina Syndrome (Medical Emergency)

Sudden loss of bowel or bladder control, numbness in the "saddle" area (groin/buttocks), or progressive bilateral leg weakness requiring immediate emergency surgery.

How is it Discovered?

A thorough clinical examination paired with targeted neuroimaging confirms the exact location and severity of the disc prolapse:

  • Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI): The definitive gold-standard diagnostic scan that clearly shows the soft tissues, detailing the exact size, direction, and nerve compression caused by the herniated disc.
  • Comprehensive Neurological Examination: A clinical evaluation checking reflexes, muscle strength, sensory loss, and leg elevation tests (Straight Leg Raise test) to identify the specific affected spinal nerve.
  • Computed Tomography (CT) Scan: High-resolution imaging used to evaluate bone anatomy and rule out calcified disc material or bone spurs when an MRI cannot be performed.
  • Electromyography (EMG) & Nerve Conduction Studies: Diagnostic tests that measure electrical activity in leg nerves to pinpoint nerve root compression and rule out peripheral nerve conditions.

How is it Treated?

Most cases of lumbar disc prolapse improve with non-surgical care, reserving high-precision microdiscectomy for persistent or severe symptoms:

  • Conservative Care & Medications: Initial management using short-term rest, short courses of anti-inflammatory medications, nerve pain modulators, and gentle activity modification.
  • Targeted Physical Therapy: Core-strengthening exercises, posture correction, and McKenzie extension exercises to relieve pressure on compressed nerve roots.
  • Lumbar Epidural Steroid Injections: Precise anti-inflammatory injections delivered under fluoroscopic guidance directly around the irritated nerve root to significantly reduce swelling and pain.
  • Microscopic/Minimally Invasive/Endoscopic Discectomy: A minimally invasive neurosurgical procedure where the surgeon uses a tiny opening to carefully remove only the protruding disc fragment, instantly relieving pressure on the nerve while preserving normal disc structure.
