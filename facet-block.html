Facet Joint Injection (Facet Block)

What Is a Facet Joint Injection?

Imagine your spine as a column of stacked bony vertebrae. While the large intervertebral discs cushion the front of the spine, paired miniature hinge joints—known as facet joints—connect the vertebrae along the back. These joints enable flexible bending, twisting, and extending while preventing your spine from slipping out of alignment. Like knees or hips, facet joints are lined with cartilage and lubricated by joint fluid.

When facet joints suffer from wear-and-tear arthritis, cartilage breakdown, repetitive strain, or traumatic injury, the joint capsule becomes inflamed and painful. This condition, known as facet joint arthropathy, causes localized aching stiffness in the neck or lower back that often radiates into the shoulders, buttocks, or thighs.

A Facet Joint Injection (Facet Block) is a precise, minimally invasive procedure. Guided by real-time fluoroscopic X-ray navigation, the pain specialist delivers a therapeutic mixture of local anesthetic and concentrated anti-inflammatory corticosteroid directly into the inflamed joint capsule (intra-articular injection) or onto the small sensory nerves (medial branches) that transmit pain signals from the joint.

Think of a facet block as applying targeted anti-inflammatory soothing directly inside an arthritic spine hinge:

  • Dual Diagnostic & Therapeutic Value: Instant pain relief from the local anesthetic confirms that the specific facet joint is the true source of pain (diagnostic), while the long-acting steroid calms joint inflammation to yield long-term pain reduction (therapeutic).
  • Precision Image Guidance: Continuous live X-ray (fluoroscopy) ensures exact needle placement inside the narrow joint space while safeguarding adjacent nerve roots.
  • Gateway to Radiofrequency Ablation: If a facet block successfully confirms pain relief, patients become candidates for longer-lasting treatments like Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA/neurotomy) to cauterize the tiny nerve fibers supplying the joint.

When Is a Facet Block Recommended?

A facet joint block is indicated when axial neck or back pain stemming from joint arthritis fails to improve with non-surgical care:

Cervical or Lumbar Facet Arthropathy

Chronic aching pain in the back of the neck or lower back caused by arthritic wear, joint inflammation, or cartilage degradation.

Pain Aggravated by Twisting or Arching Backwards

Sharp or dull pain that predictably worsens when leaning backward (extension), twisting the torso, or standing for long periods.

Referred Non-Radicular Pain

Dull aching pain that radiates into the buttocks and upper thighs (from lumbar facets) or into the shoulders and back of the head (from cervical facets), without primary nerve root compression.

Diagnostic Screening for Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

Confirming specific joint pain generators prior to performing long-term thermal nerve ablation.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Precise diagnostic mapping and clinical evaluation precede the injection:

  • Spine X-Ray, CT, or MRI Review: Neuroimaging is evaluated to identify specific facet joint hypertrophy, joint space narrowing, bone spurs, or cartilage loss corresponding to your physical exam.
  • Coagulation & Blood Thinner Assessment: Reviewing blood thinners (anticoagulants or antiplatelets) and adjusting them under medical guidance to minimize bleeding risks around joint capsules.
  • Allergy & Diabetic Screening: Screening for sensitivities to contrast dye, local anesthetics, or steroids, alongside monitoring blood glucose in diabetic patients.
  • Sterile Pre-Procedure Preparation: Performed as an outpatient procedure under local skin numbing, with light IV relaxation provided if required.

What Happens During the Procedure?

The injection takes approximately 15 to 30 minutes in a sterile fluoroscopy suite:

  • Stage 1: Position & Skin Preparation: The patient lies comfortably face-down (prone) on an X-ray transparent table. The skin over the target spine area is thoroughly cleaned and sterilized.
  • Stage 2: Local Anesthetic Infiltration: A fine needle numbs the skin and underlying soft tissues along the planned injection pathway.
  • Stage 3: Image-Guided Needle Advancement: Guided by continuous live fluoroscopic X-ray, the physician precisely advances a thin spinal needle into the intra-articular facet joint capsule (or over the bony landmarks for medial branch nerves).
  • Stage 4: Contrast Dye Verification: A microscopic droplet of contrast dye is injected to confirm that the needle tip rests accurately inside the joint capsule and has not entered surrounding blood vessels.
  • Stage 5: Therapeutic Injection & Bandage Application: The mixture of local anesthetic and long-acting corticosteroid is slowly injected into the joint. The needle is withdrawn, and a small sterile bandage is applied.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery is rapid, allowing a quick return home and rapid resumption of physical rehabilitation:

  • Immediate Recovery Monitoring (20 to 30 Minutes): Patients are monitored in the recovery area while completing a pain diary to record immediate relief provided by the local anesthetic.
  • Same-Day Outpatient Discharge: Patients return home the same day. Driving is typically restricted for the remainder of the day, but normal daily activity can be resumed the following morning.
  • Steroid Relief Timeline: While local anesthetic relief occurs instantly and lasts several hours, the anti-inflammatory corticosteroid takes 2 to 5 days to diminish joint lining inflammation and deliver sustained pain reduction.
  • Facilitating Physical Therapy: Pain relief from the block opens a therapeutic window, allowing patients to engage comfortably in core strengthening, posture training, and physical therapy.
