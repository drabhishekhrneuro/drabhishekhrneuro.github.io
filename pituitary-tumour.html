Pituitary Tumours (Pituitary Adenomas)

What Is a Pituitary Tumour?

Imagine your hormonal system as an intricate symphony orchestra. Located in a small bony pocket at the base of your brain (the sella turcica), the pea-sized pituitary gland acts as the "master conductor," producing essential hormones that control growth, metabolism, stress response, and reproductive function throughout your body.

A pituitary tumour (most commonly a benign pituitary adenoma) is an abnormal growth of tissue within this vital control center.

Think of your pituitary gland as an endocrine command hub:

  • Benign Nature: The vast majority of pituitary tumours are non-cancerous (benign adenomas) and do not spread to other organs or distant body tissues.
  • Functioning vs. Non-Functioning: Functioning tumours overproduce specific hormones (such as prolactin, growth hormone, or ACTH), leading to systemic endocrine imbalances. Non-functioning tumours do not produce hormones but cause problems by expanding and pressing on nearby structures.
  • Optic Chiasm Proximity: The pituitary gland sits directly beneath the optic nerves (where visual signals cross). As a tumour grows upward (macroadenoma), it can press on these nerves, causing characteristic visual changes.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms fall into two main categories: physical pressure on nearby brain structures (mass effect) or hormonal imbalances in the body:

Visual Disturbances (Bitemporal Hemianopia)

Loss of peripheral (side) vision or blurred sight caused by the tumor pressing upward against the optic chiasm.

Persistent Deep Headaches

Dull, pressure-type headaches behind the eyes or forehead resulting from expansion within the rigid bony sella turcica.

Hormonal Overproduction Symptoms

Unexplained weight gain, facial changes, excessive hand/foot growth (Acromegaly), irregular menstrual periods, unexpected breast milk production (galactorrhea), or easy bruising (Cushing's disease).

Hormonal Deficiency (Hypopituitarism)

Chronic fatigue, low blood pressure, unexplained cold intolerance, reduced libido, or muscle weakness caused by compression of healthy pituitary tissue.

Pituitary Apoplexy (Medical Emergency)

Sudden, severe "thunderclap" headache accompanied by rapid vision loss, double vision, or extreme lethargy caused by acute bleeding or loss of blood supply to the tumour.

How is it Discovered?

Comprehensive evaluation requires combining high-resolution neuroimaging, hormone blood panels, and specialized visual testing:

  • Dedicated Pituitary MRI: The primary gold-standard diagnostic scan using thin-slice, contrast-enhanced MRI to clearly visualize the tumour size, exact boundaries, and relationship to the optic nerves and carotid arteries.
  • Endocrine Blood & Urine Panels: Comprehensive lab tests to evaluate levels of pituitary hormones (prolactin, growth hormone, IGF-1, ACTH, cortisol, TSH, and gonadotropins) to determine if the tumour is functioning.
  • Formal Visual Field Testing: Automated perimetry performed by an ophthalmologist or neuro-ophthalmologist to assess side vision and evaluate the impact of optic nerve compression.
  • CT Scan of the Sella & Paranasal Sinuses: High-resolution CT imaging used to evaluate bony anatomy and sinus pathways in preparation for endoscopic surgical planning.

How is it Treated?

Treatment is tailored according to whether the tumour is hormone-secreting, its overall size, and whether vision is affected:

  • Medical Management (Dopamine Agonists): For prolactin-secreting tumours (prolactinomas), targeted oral medications (such as Cabergoline or Bromocriptine) are typically the first-line treatment, effectively shrinking the tumour and restoring normal hormone levels without surgery.
  • Endoscopic Endonasal Transsphenoidal Surgery: A modern, minimally invasive neurosurgical procedure where the surgeon accesses and removes the tumour through the nose and sphenoid sinus using HD endoscopes, avoiding external incisions or brain retraction.
  • Stereotactic Radiosurgery (Gamma Knife / CyberKnife): Highly focused precision radiation delivered to small, persistent, or recurrent tumour remnants to control growth while protecting surrounding optic nerves.
  • Hormone Replacement Therapy: Specialized medical management supervised by an endocrinologist to restore deficient hormone levels if normal pituitary function is impaired.
  • Long-Term Endocrine & MRI Surveillance: Regular clinical visits, hormone checks, and MRI scans to ensure long-term control and detect any subtle recurrences early.
