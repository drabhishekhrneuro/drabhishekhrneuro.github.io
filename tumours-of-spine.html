Tumours of Spine

Spine Tumours

What Is a Spine Tumour?

Imagine your spine as a robust, protective column made of interlocked bones (vertebrae) enclosing a delicate electrical trunk line—the spinal cord—and its exiting nerve roots. This central pathway transmits vital signals between your brain and the rest of your body.

A spine tumour is an abnormal growth of cells that develops either within the bony structures of the spine, inside the protective spinal canal, or directly within the delicate spinal cord tissue itself.

Think of the spine as a high-security structural conduit:

  • Primary vs. Secondary (Metastatic): Primary spine tumours originate directly within the spinal bones or nerve structures. Secondary (metastatic) spine tumours—which are far more common—spread from cancers in other parts of the body, such as the lungs, breast, prostate, or kidneys.
  • Location-Based Classification: Spine tumours are broadly categorized by where they sit relative to the spinal cord and its protective outer membrane (dura):
    • Extradural: Located outside the dura within the vertebral bones or epidural space (most common, often metastatic).
    • Intradural-Extramedullary: Located inside the dura but outside the spinal cord tissue (such as schwannomas, neurofibromas, or meningiomas).
    • Intramedullary: Located directly inside the tissue of the spinal cord itself (such as astrocytomas or ependymomas).
  • Structural & Neurological Compression: As a tumour expands, it can weaken the surrounding bone—leading to spinal instability or fractures—or press directly on the spinal cord and nerve roots.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms depend on the location, size, and type of spine tumour, as well as whether it causes local bone weakening or direct nerve/spinal cord compression:

Non-Mechanical Night Pain

Persistent, deep back or neck pain that worsens at night, is not relieved by rest or lying down, and may wake you from sleep.

Radiating Nerve Pain (Radiculopathy)

Sharp, electric shock-like or burning pain shooting around the chest wall or down into an arm or leg along compressed nerve pathways.

Progressive Numbness, Tingling, or Weakness

"Pins and needles," loss of temperature sensation, or worsening muscle weakness in the arms, hands, legs, or feet.

Gait Instability & Coordination Loss (Myelopathy)

Unsteadiness while walking, feeling clumsy or heavy-legged, or losing fine motor skills in the hands due to central spinal cord pressure.

Loss of Bowel or Bladder Control

Unexpected changes in bowel or bladder function or progressive saddle-area numbness, which represents a critical medical emergency requiring immediate evaluation.

How is it Discovered?

Accurate evaluation requires precise neuroimaging to pinpoint the tumor's exact anatomical compartment, evaluate structural stability, and determine its origin:

  • Contrast-Enhanced Spine MRI: The definitive gold-standard diagnostic tool. Multi-planar MRI with contrast clearly shows the spinal cord, nerve roots, soft tissue extension, and exact borders of the tumour.
  • High-Resolution CT Scan & CT Angiography: Detailed bony imaging used to assess vertebral erosion, bone destruction, pathological fractures, and tumor blood supply for surgical planning.
  • Systemic Staging Scans (PET-CT / Chest-Abdomen-Pelvis CT): Comprehensive body imaging performed when a secondary (metastatic) tumor is suspected, helping locate the primary source or other lesions.
  • Percutaneous or Intraoperative Biopsy: Collecting a small tissue sample for microscopic, histopathological, and molecular analysis to guide specific targeted medical, radiation, or surgical therapies.

How is it Treated?

Treatment is highly individualized and coordinated by a multidisciplinary team based on tumor type, location, spinal stability, and neurological symptoms:

  • Microsurgical Resection / Decompression: High-precision neurosurgery performed with high-power operating microscopes, ultrasonic aspirators, and real-time intraoperative neuromonitoring (SSEP/MEP) to carefully remove the tumour or unpinch the spinal cord while protecting nerve function.
  • Spinal Stabilization & Reconstruction: Placing supportive pedicle screws, rods, or vertebral body spacers to restore alignment and structural strength when a tumour has destroyed supporting spinal bones.
  • Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT / CyberKnife): Advanced precision radiation technology delivering high-dose, targeted beams to destroy spinal tumours with sub-millimeter accuracy while sparing the adjacent sensitive spinal cord.
  • Vertebroplasty or Kyphoplasty: A minimally invasive procedure where specialized medical bone cement is injected into a fractured or tumor-weakened vertebra to stabilize the bone and provide immediate pain relief.
  • Targeted Medical & Systemic Therapies: Corticosteroids to rapidly reduce acute spinal cord swelling, alongside chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or hormone therapy tailored to the specific tumor profile.
