Ischemic Stroke

What Is an Ischemic Stroke?

Imagine your brain’s blood vessels as a busy network of pipes supplying continuous oxygen and nutrients to millions of active brain cells. Under normal conditions, clean, oxygen-rich blood flows freely through these channels to keep brain tissue healthy and functioning smoothly.

An ischemic stroke occurs when a blood vessel carrying blood to the brain becomes suddenly blocked by a blood clot or a buildup of fatty plaque.

Because brain cells rely on a constant supply of oxygen, a sudden blockage causes the affected brain region to starve. Without rapid treatment to clear the blockage and restore blood flow, brain cells in the starved area begin to suffer damage within minutes, leading to sudden neurological deficits.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For (BE FAST)

Recognizing the sudden onset of stroke symptoms is critical. Remember the acronym BE FAST to identify early signs of an ischemic stroke:

B – Balance Loss

Sudden dizziness, difficulty walking, loss of coordination, or unsteadiness.

E – Eyesight Changes

Sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes, blurred sight, or double vision.

F – Facial Drooping

One side of the face sags or feels numb, resulting in an uneven smile.

A – Arm or Leg Weakness

Sudden weakness, heaviness, or numbness in an arm or leg, usually on one side of the body.

S – Speech Difficulty

Slurred speech, inability to speak, or trouble understanding simple spoken sentences.

T – Time to Call Emergency Services

Emergency medical care is required immediately. Every minute saved preserves brain tissue.

How is it Discovered?

Rapid, high-priority neuroimaging is essential to distinguish an ischemic stroke from a brain hemorrhage and evaluate options for restoring blood flow:

  • Non-Contrast CT Scan: The immediate emergency scan used to quickly rule out a brain hemorrhage (bleeding) and assess initial brain structures.
  • CT Angiography (CTA) & Perfusion CT: Specialized rapid imaging that maps out major neck and brain arteries to locate large blood vessel blockages (Large Vessel Occlusions) and assess recoverable brain tissue.
  • Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) & MRA: High-resolution imaging (such as Diffusion-Weighted Imaging) that detects microscopic early brain cell changes from ischemia in high detail.
  • Carotid Ultrasound & Echocardiogram: Secondary diagnostic tests to identify the source of the clot, checking for carotid artery plaque or heart-related clot sources (like atrial fibrillation).

How is it Treated?

Emergency treatment focuses on rapidly dissolving or physically removing the blood clot to restore blood flow to starving brain tissue:

  • Intravenous Thrombolysis (Clot-Busting Medication): Time-sensitive IV medication (such as tPA or Tenecteplase) administered within the early hours of symptom onset to dissolve the blood clot.
  • Mechanical Thrombectomy: A specialized, minimally invasive neurointerventional procedure where a fine catheter is guided through the blood vessels to physically grab and remove a large clot from a blocked brain artery using a stent retriever or suction device.
  • Antiplatelet & Anticoagulant Therapy: Medications (like aspirin or blood thinners) initiated to prevent new clots from forming or existing ones from enlarging.
  • Stroke Unit Care & Rehabilitation: Dedicated post-acute medical management focusing on blood pressure optimization, swallowing assessments, and early physical, speech, and occupational therapy to maximize functional recovery.
