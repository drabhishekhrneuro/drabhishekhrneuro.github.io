Spine & Disc Disorders

Lumbar Spondylolisthesis

What Is Lumbar Spondylolisthesis?

Imagine your spine as a stacked column of interlocked building blocks (vertebrae) perfectly aligned to support your body's weight while allowing flexible movement. Each bone is held in place by small alignment joints (facet joints), strong ligaments, and supportive intervertebral discs.

Lumbar spondylolisthesis occurs when one vertebra slips forward out of its natural position over the bone directly beneath it in the lower back.

Think of your lower back column as experiencing a structural shift:

  • Structural Misalignment: When a vertebra slips forward, it disrupts the smooth, vertical alignment of the spinal column.
  • Nerve Narrowing (Foraminal & Central Stenosis): As the top bone slides forward, it drags against the bone below, narrowing the central spinal canal and pinching the exit windows (foramina) where nerve roots travel to your legs.
  • Causes & Types: It commonly results from age-related wear and tear in the facet joints (degenerative spondylolisthesis) or from a stress fracture in the small connecting bone bridge (isthmic spondylolisthesis).

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms depend on the degree of vertebral slip and whether shifting bones are compressing spinal nerves or causing joint instability:

Mechanical Lower Back Pain

A deep, persistent ache in the lower back that worsens with standing, walking, or leaning backward (extending the spine) and improves when resting or bending forward.

Sciatica & Radiating Leg Pain

Sharp, burning, or electric shock-like pain traveling down into the buttocks, thighs, or calves due to nerve root pinching at the slip site.

Hamstring Tightness & Stiffness

Tightness in the back of the thighs and general stiffness in the lower back, causing a shortened stride or altered walking gait.

Numbness, Tingling, or Leg Weakness

"Pins and needles" sensation or reduced strength in the legs or feet, leading to feeling unsteady while walking or standing.

Visible Spine Deformity or Saddle Anesthesia

In severe high-grade slips, a noticeable "step-off" bump on the lower back, or sudden bowel/bladder changes with saddle-area numbness (Cauda Equina Syndrome), which requires immediate emergency medical evaluation.

How is it Discovered?

Confirming spondylolisthesis requires evaluating both static alignment and dynamic movement of the lower spine:

  • Dynamic Flexion-Extension X-rays: The primary initial diagnostic tool. Standard standing X-rays combined with bending forward and backward views assess whether the slipped bone moves abnormally (spinal instability).
  • Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI): The gold-standard scan to evaluate soft tissues, clearly showing the extent of nerve root compression, disc health, and canal narrowing caused by the slip.
  • Computed Tomography (CT) Scan: High-resolution bone imaging used to visualize fine bony structures, stress fractures in the pars interarticularis, and joint fusion status.

How is it Treated?

Treatment ranges from core stabilization to neurosurgical procedures designed to unpinch nerves and restore spinal stability:

  • Conservative Management & Physical Therapy: Focused core-strengthening, hamstring stretching, abdominal exercises, and temporary bracing to support the lower back and reduce mechanical strain.
  • Epidural Steroid Injections: Targeted anti-inflammatory medications injected near compressed nerve roots under fluoroscopic guidance to relieve severe radiating leg pain.
  • Decompression Surgery (Laminectomy): A procedure to remove bone spurs and thickened tissue to free pinched spinal nerves when symptoms are primarily caused by nerve crowding.
  • Instrumented Spinal Fusion (TLIF / PLIF): The definitive surgical treatment for unstable slips. Pedicle screws and rods stabilize the vertebrae while a interbody spacer is placed to restore disc height, align the spine, and prevent further slippage.
