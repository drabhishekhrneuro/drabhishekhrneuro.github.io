Subdural Empyema

What Is a Subdural Empyema?

A subdural empyema is a collection of pus that builds up in the space between the outer protective lining of the brain (the dura) and the brain itself.

Unlike a brain abscess—which is a closed "pocket" inside the brain tissue—a subdural empyema forms in an open layer, allowing infected fluid to spread rapidly over the surface of the brain. It is a severe medical emergency that requires immediate hospital treatment.

Think of the skull and brain like a hard-cover book with a plastic wrap lining:

  • The Gap Under the Lining: Just under your skull sits a tough protective sheet called the dura. Below it is a narrow, natural gap (the subdural space) lining the outside of the brain.
  • The Uncontained Spread: When bacteria enter this gap, there are no internal walls to block them. The infection can spread like spilled liquid across the surface of the brain.
  • The Double Threat: As fluid builds up, it creates dangerous pressure against the brain while simultaneously causing nearby blood vessels to swell or clot, cutting off healthy blood flow.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms often start like a severe sinus or ear infection and then escalate very rapidly over a few days:

High Fever and Chills

A spiking temperature as the body tries to fight off a widespread infection.

Rapidly Worsening Severe Headache

An intense, continuous headache that gets worse very quickly and does not respond to painkillers.

Confusion and Lethargy

Becoming unusually sleepy, confused or hard to wake up as pressure builds over the brain.

Stiff Neck

Discomfort or resistance when trying to bend your chin down toward your chest, similar to meningitis.

Weakness or Seizures

Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, slurred speech or unprovoked shaking fits (seizures).

How Infection Gets There

Bacteria usually gain access to the subdural space through one of three primary routes:

  • Complication of Sinus or Ear Infections: Most commonly spreads directly from untreated or severe frontal sinus infections or middle ear/mastoid infections.
  • Head Trauma or Surgery: Bacteria entering directly following a skull injury, severe fracture or neurosurgical procedure.
  • Spreading Blood Clots: Infection traveling through tiny veins that connect the face or sinuses directly to the brain's outer lining.

Emergency Care & Treatment

Because pus can spread quickly over a wide area, emergency surgery is almost always required immediately upon diagnosis, alongside intensive medical management:

  • Intravenous (IV) Antibiotics: Immediate high-dose antibiotics given through a vein to fight the specific bacteria causing the infection.
  • Anti-Seizure & Anti-Swelling Medications: Drugs to stabilize brain activity and reduce swelling on the surface of the brain.
  • Surgical Indications: As soon as an MRI or CT scan confirms a subdural empyema, emergency surgery is planned to relieve pressure and prevent permanent brain damage or stroke.
  • Surgical Evacuation (Craniotomy or Burr Holes): A neurosurgeon creates a small opening in the skull to wash out the infected fluid, flush the area with sterile liquid, and place temporary drains. Removing this trapped fluid instantly takes dangerous pressure off the brain.
