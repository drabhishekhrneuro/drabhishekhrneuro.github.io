Minimally Invasive Spine Surgery

Balloon Kyphoplasty

What Is Balloon Kyphoplasty?

Imagine your spine as a column of stacked, box-like bones called vertebrae that provide structural support and enclose the spinal cord. When osteoporosis, traumatic impact, or bone-weakening conditions weaken these vertebral bodies, the front portion of the bone can collapse under normal daily stress—a condition known as a Vertebral Compression Fracture (VCF). This collapse causes severe localized back pain, height loss, and a progressive forward stoop (kyphosis).

Balloon Kyphoplasty is a minimally invasive, image-guided spine procedure performed through keyhole punctures in the back. Using high-precision needles, small inflatable orthopedic balloons are guided inside the fractured, collapsed vertebra. Inflating the balloons gently expands the compressed bone, restoring height and creating a internal cavity that is then filled with specialized medical-grade bone cement to permanently stabilize the fracture.

Think of kyphoplasty as using an internal inflatable jack to restore a collapsed building block before filling it with concrete:

  • Vertebral Height Restoration: Unlike simple vertebroplasty, inflating the orthopedic balloon physically lifts the collapsed endplates back toward their original height, helping correct painful spine deformity.
  • Controlled Cement Cavity Creation: Compacting the surrounding spongy bone with the balloon creates a low-pressure chamber for cement injection, significantly reducing the risk of leakage into surrounding nerve spaces.
  • Immediate Structural Stabilization: Rapid-hardening acrylic bone cement (PMMA) solidifies within minutes, providing instantaneous structural support and immediate relief from fracture pain.

When Is Kyphoplasty Recommended?

Kyphoplasty is indicated for painful, acute or subacute vertebral compression fractures that do not improve with medical therapy:

Painful Osteoporotic Compression Fractures

Severe, debilitating mid-back or lower-back pain caused by bone loss and minor trauma, where standing, walking, or turning over in bed causes intense sharp pain.

Pathological Fractures from Tumors or Myeloma

Vertebral body collapse resulting from spinal metastases or multiple myeloma, providing structural reinforcement and immediate pain relief.

Progressive Spinal Deformity (Kyphosis)

Recent fractures leading to measurable loss of height and progressive forward spinal curvature ("dowager's hump").

Failure of Non-Surgical Treatment

Severe pain that persists despite 2 to 4 weeks of conservative management, including bed rest, rigid bracing, and prescription pain medications.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Targeted imaging confirms fracture acuity and structural integrity prior to intervention:

  • Thoracic and Lumbar Spine MRI (with STIR Sequences): The gold-standard scan to confirm bone marrow edema (swelling), proving the fracture is fresh/acute and responsible for the patient's pain.
  • Fine-Cut Spine CT Scan: Evaluates the posterior wall of the vertebral body to confirm it is intact, ensuring safe cement containment during injection.
  • Dynamic Spine X-Rays: Quantifies the exact percentage of vertebral body height loss and evaluates regional kyphotic angle deformity.
  • Bone Density (DEXA) & Coagulation Screening: Assesses underlying systemic osteoporosis and verifies normal blood clotting parameters.

What Happens During the Procedure?

The operation is performed under local anesthesia with conscious sedation (or general anesthesia) through precise keyhole steps:

  • Stage 1: Position & Fluoroscopic Guidance: The patient lies face-down (prone) on a padded table. High-resolution 2D/3D X-ray fluoroscopy is positioned over the precise back level.
  • Stage 2: Keyhole Needle Access: Two tiny skin punctures (approx. 3 to 5 mm) are made in the back. Under continuous real-time X-ray guidance, narrow hollow needles (cannulas) are passed safely through the bony pedicles into the collapsed vertebral body.
  • Stage 3: Balloon Insertion & Height Restoration: Inflatable orthopedic balloons are passed through the working channels into the center of the bone. The surgeon slowly inflates the balloons under precise pressure monitoring, lifting the collapsed endplates back to normal height.
  • Stage 4: Balloon Deflation & Cement Filling: The balloons are deflated and withdrawn, leaving a clean, compact cavity. Viscous, medical-grade PMMA bone cement is gently injected under low pressure into the void under continuous fluoroscopy.
  • Stage 5: Hardening & Puncture Closure: The cement hardens completely within 10 to 15 minutes. Instruments are removed, and the tiny skin punctures are covered with small adhesive bandages—no stitches required.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following kyphoplasty offers near-instantaneous relief and rapid return to mobility:

  • Same-Day Discharge (Outpatient Procedure): Patients are monitored in recovery for 1 to 2 hours while the cement sets fully and are typically discharged home the same day.
  • Immediate Pain Relief & Walking: Most patients report immediate, dramatic reduction in sharp fracture pain upon standing and walking shortly after the procedure.
  • Wound Care & Minimal Activity Restrictions: Incision sites heal within days under simple bandages. Light daily activities can be resumed immediately; heavy lifting (over 10 lbs) is restricted for 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Long-Term Osteoporosis Management: Comprehensive treatment for underlying osteoporosis (calcium, vitamin D, and anti-resorptive medications) is continued to protect adjacent vertebrae from future fractures.
