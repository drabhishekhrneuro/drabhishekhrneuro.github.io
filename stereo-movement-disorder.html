Stereotactic Functional Surgery for Movement Disorders (DBS & Lesioning)

What Is Stereotactic Surgery for Movement Disorders?

Imagine the brain's internal motor circuit as a complex electrical network. In movement disorders like Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, or dystonia, specific deep brain nuclei (such as the subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus) malfunction, firing chaotic electrical signals that manifest as debilitating tremors, rigidity, slowness, or involuntary muscle contractions.

Stereotactic functional surgery is an advanced, precision-guided neurosurgical procedure designed to target these microscopic deep brain structures. Using high-resolution 3D coordinate mapping combined with microelectrode recording, neurosurgeons can either implant a continuous neuromodulation device (**Deep Brain Stimulation - DBS**) or perform precise, targeted thermal ablation (**Stereotactic Lesioning / MRgFUS**) to reset the brain's motor circuit and restore smooth movement.

Think of this procedure as high-precision electrical tuning for abnormal brain circuits:

  • Sub-Millimeter Deep Brain Targeting: Stereotactic frames and frameless computer navigation allow surgeons to navigate deep subcortical structures with pinpoint accuracy.
  • Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS): Implantation of microscopic electrodes connected to a chest pacemaker that delivers continuous electrical impulses to block abnormal tremor signals.
  • Reversible vs. Permanent Options: DBS settings can be adjusted non-invasively over time to match changing symptom needs, while targeted ablative procedures provide immediate, permanent lesioning for specific tremors.

When Is Functional Stereotactic Surgery Recommended?

Surgery is recommended for patients whose movement disorder symptoms are no longer adequately controlled by optimized medications:

Medication-Refractory Parkinson's Disease

Patients experiencing disabling motor fluctuations, severe tremors, unpredictable "off" periods, or medication-induced dyskinesias.

Medically Intractable Essential Tremor

Severe, progressive tremors in the hands or head that interfere significantly with eating, writing, drinking, and daily independence.

Primary Dystonia & Generalized Spasms

Painful, sustained muscle contractions and twisting postures that impair mobility and fail to respond to oral medications or botulinum toxin injections.

Good Overall Cognitive & Medical Fitness

Patients evaluated and cleared by a multidisciplinary team (neurologist, neurosurgeon, neuropsychologist) confirming appropriate surgical candidacy.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Comprehensive multidisciplinary assessment and advanced imaging are critical to successful functional neurosurgery:

  • High-Resolution Stereotactic Brain MRI (T1, T2, & DTI Tractography): Specialized thin-slice MRI scans visualizing microscopic deep brain targets (STN, GPi, Vim nucleus) and surrounding white-matter tracts.
  • Multidisciplinary Board Evaluation: Rigorous screening by movement disorder neurologists, neurosurgeons, and neuropsychologists to confirm diagnosis and optimize pre-operative medication regimens.
  • Stereotactic Frame or Fiducial Planning: Mapping exact 3D stereotactic coordinates to calculate safe, precise surgical trajectories that avoid ventricles and blood vessels.
  • Awake vs. Asleep DBS Protocols: Tailoring the intraoperative approach (either performing microelectrode recording in an awake patient or utilizing high-field intraoperative MRI under general anesthesia).

What Happens During the Surgery?

The procedure is executed with meticulous precision through specialized stereotactic phases:

  • Stage 1: Stereotactic Frame Placement & Imaging: A lightweight stereotactic head frame is secured comfortably under local anesthesia, followed by a planning CT or MRI scan to verify 3D coordinates.
  • Stage 2: Precision Burr Hole & Trajectory: Through tiny coin-sized burr holes, a specialized micro-instrument or DBS electrode is guided precisely along the planned trajectory into the deep target nucleus.
  • Stage 3: Electrophysiological Verification (If Awake): Microelectrode recording listens to the unique firing patterns of brain cells, and test stimulation is performed while checking tremor and rigidity in real time.
  • Stage 4: Permanent Electrode or Ablation Delivery: Once optimal placement is confirmed, the permanent DBS lead is anchored securely to the skull. (For battery placement, a second small incision connects the lead under the skin down to a pacemaker generator in the chest wall).
  • Stage 5: Verification & Closure: Post-procedure imaging confirms perfect lead placement, and all small incisions are closed with meticulous sutures.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery focuses on healing, programming adjustments, and reclaiming functional independence:

  • Post-Op Neuro-Imaging: A brain CT or MRI scan is performed within 24 hours to verify exact lead placement and ensure complete safety.
  • DBS Programming Sessions: For DBS patients, the neurostimulator is turned on and programmed in the clinic a few weeks post-op, slowly fine-tuning electrical settings to achieve maximal tremor and rigidity control while minimizing side effects.
  • Medication Optimization: As electrical stimulation takes over motor control, the movement disorder neurologist gradually adjusts oral medications.
  • Long-Term Follow-Up: Regular clinical check-ups and battery longevity monitoring ensure sustained, long-term improvement in quality of life and motor function.
