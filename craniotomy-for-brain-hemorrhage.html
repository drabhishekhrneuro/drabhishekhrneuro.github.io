Craniotomy for Brain Hemorrhage (Intracerebral Hemorrhage)

What Is a Craniotomy for Brain Hemorrhage?

Imagine your brain cradled within a rigid, closed bone vault. When a weakened blood vessel ruptures deep inside the brain tissue—due to chronic high blood pressure, vascular malformations, or trauma—blood pours rapidly into a confined space, forming an expanding clot (hematoma). This blood collection acts like an aggressive mass, crushing surrounding brain cells and driving intracranial pressure dangerously high.

A craniotomy for brain hemorrhage is an open, life-saving neurosurgical procedure where a temporary section of the skull bone is removed to directly access, evacuate, and clean out a large intracerebral blood clot, relieving catastrophic pressure off the brain.

Think of this procedure as direct surgical intervention to relieve a dangerous internal pressure crisis:

  • Direct Clot Removal & Decompression: Unlike needle aspirations or burr holes, an open craniotomy gives the neurosurgeon direct visual control to remove large, solid, or multi-loculated blood clots that threaten brainstem function.
  • Source Control & Hemostasis: Working under high-power operating microscopes, the surgeon can identify and coagulate bleeding blood vessels or address underlying vascular lesions (such as aneurysms or AVMs) if present.
  • Mitigating Secondary Brain Injury: Removing the hematoma eliminates toxic blood breakdown products and immediately restores normal blood flow and oxygenation to surrounding stressed brain tissue.

When Is a Craniotomy Recommended for Hemorrhage?

Surgery is recommended when a brain hemorrhage is large enough, or located in a position where mass effect threatens survival or causes progressive neurological decline:

Large Lobar Intracerebral Hemorrhages

Clots located close to the surface of the brain lobes (frontal, temporal, parietal, or occipital) that cause severe midline shift, altered consciousness, or progressive paralysis.

Life-Threatening Cerebellar Hemorrhage

Bleeding in the back of the brain (cerebellum) that rapidly compresses the brainstem and fourth ventricle, requiring urgent surgical evacuation to prevent respiratory arrest.

Rapid Neurological Deterioration

When a patient experiences unresponsiveness, pupil changes, or signs of impending brain herniation despite intensive medical management.

Hemorrhages Linked to Structural Lesions

Bleeding caused by underlying ruptured aneurysms, arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), or cavernous malformations that require simultaneous surgical removal of the lesion.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Emergency preoperative assessment focuses on stabilizing systemic vitals and mapping the exact dimensions of the bleed:

  • Emergency Non-Contrast Brain CT & CTA/DSA: Rapid imaging to measure clot volume, assess brain swelling and midline shift, and rule out underlying vascular malformations or aneurysms.
  • Coagulation & Blood Thinner Reversal: Urgent laboratory testing to evaluate clotting function and immediately administer reversal agents if the patient was taking blood thinners (anticoagulants or antiplatelets).
  • Systemic & Blood Pressure Stabilization: Securing airway protection (intubation) and managing blood pressure parameters under critical care supervision prior to surgery.
  • Neuronavigation & Trajectory Mapping: Integrating scan data into computer-assisted surgical navigation to plan the safest, most direct surgical corridor through the brain tissue.

What Happens During the Surgery?

The procedure is executed under general anesthesia through precise, life-saving microsurgical stages:

  • Stage 1: Scalp Incision & Craniotomy: Under general anesthesia, a tailored scalp incision is made, and a bone flap is opened directly over the hemorrhage using neuronavigation guidance.
  • Stage 2: Dural Opening & Corticotomy: The protective outer brain membrane (dura) is opened. A tiny, safe entry point (corticotomy) is made through the brain tissue overlying the edge of the blood clot.
  • Stage 3: Microsurgical Clot Evacuation: Working under high-power operating microscope magnification, the neurosurgeon systematically removes the clotted blood using gentle micro-suction, ultrasonic aspirators, and warm saline irrigation.
  • Stage 4: Hemostasis & Vessel Inspection: Once the cavity is cleared of blood, the walls are inspected to ensure all active bleeding is stopped using delicate bipolar electrocautery.
  • Stage 5: Closure & Bone Management: The dura is closed, and the bone flap is reattached (or left off for delayed replacement if severe brain swelling is present). The scalp is closed with sutures or staples.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following a craniotomy for brain hemorrhage requires intensive care management, blood pressure control, and structured rehabilitation:

  • Neuro-ICU Life Support (First 24–72 Hours): Continuous monitoring in a specialized neuro-intensive care unit for blood pressure regulation, intracranial pressure tracking, and neurological assessments.
  • Post-Operative CT Imaging: A repeat brain CT scan is performed within 24 hours of surgery to confirm successful evacuation of the hematoma and check for any residual swelling or bleeding.
  • Blood Pressure & Medical Optimization: Strict maintenance of target blood pressure ranges to protect delicate brain tissue and prevent re-bleeding or ischemia.
  • Comprehensive Rehabilitation Therapy: Physical, occupational, and speech therapy programs are initiated as soon as the patient is medically stable to support long-term functional recovery and independence.
