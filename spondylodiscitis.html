Spondylodiscitis

What Is Spondylodiscitis?

Spondylodiscitis is a serious infection that affects the spine—specifically targeting the vertebrae (the bones of your spine) and the soft intervertebral discs (the rubbery shock-absorbing cushions between them).

Because the infection involves both the bone (spondylitis) and the adjacent disc (discitis), it causes significant local inflammation, severe pain and swelling within the spinal canal.

Think of your spine as a stack of building blocks separated by gel-filled pads:

  • The Entry Point: Bacteria or fungi enter your bloodstream from an infection elsewhere in your body (or directly during a procedure) and settle into the tiny blood vessels supplying the spine.
  • The Spread: The infection usually starts inside a "gel-filled pad" (the disc). Because discs have limited blood flow, your body's immune system has a hard time reaching the area quickly, allowing the infection to grow and spread into the neighboring upper and lower "building blocks" (vertebrae).
  • The Damage & Pressure: As the bone and disc erode from the infection, surrounding tissue swells. If left untreated, this swelling can bulge backward into the spinal canal, pressing against sensitive nerve roots or the spinal cord itself.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms typically start gradually over weeks and can easily be mistaken for standard mechanical back pain at first:

Severe, Continuous Back Pain

A deep, intense ache in a specific spot along your spine (most commonly the lower back) that persists even when resting or lying down and often worsens at night.

Tenderness to Touch

Sharp pain when gently tapping or pressing directly over the affected section of your spine.

Fever, Chills, & Night Sweats

A lingering temperature, feeling unusually cold or shivering or waking up drenched in sweat as your body fights the infection.

Stiffness & Guarding

Extreme difficulty bending, twisting or standing straight because the surrounding back muscles lock up to protect the inflamed area.

Shooting Pain or Numbness

Sharp, electric-like pain shooting down into your legs or arms if swelling presses on neighboring nerve roots.

Common Causes & Risk Factors

Spondylodiscitis can arise from circulating bloodstream pathogens, spinal procedures, or underlying health vulnerabilities:

  • Bloodstream Spread: Bacteria (most commonly Staphylococcus aureus, or Mycobacterium tuberculosis in cases of spinal TB) traveling from skin infections, dental work, urinary tract infections or intravenous (IV) lines.
  • Recent Procedures or Trauma: Complications following spinal surgery, epidural injections or direct back trauma.
  • Underlying Health Conditions: Higher risk in individuals with diabetes, kidney disease, weakened immune systems or an older age.

Medical Care & Treatment

Most cases heal successfully with conservative medical management, immobilization, and long-term antimicrobial therapy:

  • Long-Course Antibiotics or Antifungals: Extended targeted intravenous (IV) medication—often given for 6 to 12 weeks—to clear the infection completely out of dense bone tissue.
  • Spinal Immobilization: Wearing a rigid back or neck brace to stabilize the spine, minimize pain with movement and prevent bone collapse while healing.
  • Targeted Pain Relief: Anti-inflammatory medications and pain relievers to manage discomfort during initial recovery.
  • Surgical Indications: Surgery is evaluated if the infection causes severe bone destruction leading to spinal collapse, if a large pus collection (abscess) forms, or if swelling presses on the spinal cord, causing leg weakness or loss of bowel/bladder control.
  • Surgical Debridement & Stabilization: A spine surgeon performs a procedure to clean out (debride) infected tissue and pus, relieve pressure on the spinal cord, and place supportive hardware (screws or cages) to stabilize the spine bones so they heal securely.
