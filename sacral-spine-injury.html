Sacral Spine Injury

What Is a Sacral Spine Injury?

A sacral spine injury affects the sacrum—the large, shield-shaped bone sitting at the very base of your spine, tucked right between your hip bones above the tailbone.

Unlike the individual bones in your upper back or lower back, the sacrum is made of five bones that fused together into one solid wedge. It acts as the ultimate bridge transferring weight from your spine down into your pelvis and legs.

Think of your sacrum as the keystone in a stone archway:

  • The Weight-Bearing Base: Every time you sit, stand or walk, your entire upper body weight presses down onto this single central bone.
  • The Connection Joints: It attaches to your hip bones on either side via the sacroiliac (SI) joints—held together by some of the strongest ligaments in the human body.
  • The Highway of Nerves: Important nerve bundles exit through small openings in the sacrum to control your hips, legs, pelvic floor and bowel/bladder functions.
  • The Injury: A hard impact or repetitive stress can strain these heavy-duty ligaments, misalign the SI joint or cause hairline cracks (stress fractures) in the bone itself.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms of a sacral spine injury focus heavily on deep pelvic, hip, and buttock discomfort during weight-bearing activities:

Deep Buttock Pain

A localized, aching pain deep in the buttocks or upper hips rather than high up on the back.

Pain When Sitting Down

Sitting in a chair feels like sitting directly on a sharp bruise or golf ball, making you want to lean to one side to relieve pressure.

Discomfort When Standing on One Leg

Activities that put weight on a single leg—like walking up stairs, putting on pants or getting out of a car—trigger a sharp jab in the hip/buttock area.

Thigh or Groin Radiation

A dull ache that travels down into the back of the thigh or around into the groin area.

Common Triggers

Sacral injuries typically result from direct impacts, repetitive physical strain, or hormonal ligament shifts:

  • Hard Falls: Landing hard on your bottom (e.g., slipping on ice, falling off a ladder or sports falls).
  • High-Impact Accidents: Rear-end vehicle collisions or motorcycle accidents where severe pressure is forced through the hips.
  • Overuse / Stress Fractures: Repetitive impact from long-distance running or bone density loss (like osteoporosis) making the sacrum vulnerable to small cracks.
  • Pregnancy & Childbirth: Pregnancy hormones loosen the sacral ligaments, making the joints unstable and prone to strain.

How Is It Treated & Managed?

Most sacral strains and stress cracks heal effectively with conservative, supportive care and targeted pelvic stabilization:

  • Targeted Rest: Avoiding prolonged sitting on hard surfaces; donut cushions or wedge pillows help take direct pressure off the bone.
  • Sacroiliac (SI) Belt: A tight, supportive belt worn around the hips to stabilize the joints while strained ligaments heal.
  • Physical Therapy: Exercises focused on strengthening the deep core and glute muscles to stabilize the pelvic bridge.
  • Surgical Evaluation: Surgery (like using small pins or screws to stabilize the bone or SI joint) is typically reserved for severe traumatic fractures—like those from major vehicle collisions—or chronic joint instability that hasn't responded to non-surgical care.
