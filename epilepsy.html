Epilepsy

What Is Epilepsy?

Imagine your brain as an immensely complex electrical network, where billions of neurons communicate using balanced electrical impulses to control thoughts, movement, sensation, and awareness. Under normal conditions, these electrical signals travel along organized, predictable pathways.

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological condition characterized by a tendency for recurrent, unprovoked electrical surges in the brain, known as seizures.

Think of your brain's network as experiencing a localized electrical surge:

  • Electrical Hypersynchrony: A seizure occurs when a group of brain cells fires off excessive, abnormal, and synchronized electrical discharges all at once, temporarily disrupting normal brain function.
  • Focal vs. Generalized Origin: Seizures may begin in one specific region or structural focus within the brain (focal seizures) or involve electrical networks across both hemispheres simultaneously (generalized seizures).
  • Structural & Functional Causes: While some epilepsy cases are genetic or idiopathic, many stem from identifiable structural changes—such as mesial temporal sclerosis (hippocampal scarring), brain scars from past trauma or stroke, cavernomas, cortical dysplasia, or low-grade tumours.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms depend heavily on where the abnormal electrical activity originates in the brain and how far it spreads during an episode:

Sensory or Emotional "Aura"

An early warning sign before a seizure, such as an unusual rising feeling in the stomach, sudden inexplicable fear or joy, strange metallic tastes, distorted smells, or déjà vu sensations.

Focal Unaware Seizures (Staring Spells)

Brief periods of altered awareness where a person stares blankly into space, becomes unresponsive, or performs repetitive unorganized movements (automations) like lip-smacking, chewing, or hand-fumbling.

Generalized Tonic-Clonic Seizures (Convulsions)

Sudden loss of consciousness accompanied by body stiffening (tonic phase) followed by rhythmic jerking of the arms and legs (clonic phase), often with tongue biting or temporary loss of bladder control.

Post-Ictal Confusion & Fatigue

A recovery period immediately following a seizure characterized by deep confusion, headache, muscle soreness, fatigue, or temporary speech difficulties.

Status Epilepticus (Medical Emergency)

A continuous seizure lasting longer than 5 minutes or multiple seizures occurring back-to-back without regaining consciousness in between, requiring immediate emergency medical intervention.

How is it Discovered?

Accurately mapping the epileptogenic zone (the precise region of the brain causing seizures) requires multi-modal neurological testing:

  • Video-Electroencephalogram (Video-EEG) Monitoring: Continuous long-term recording of brain electrical activity alongside simultaneous video footage to capture, characterize, and pinpoint seizure origins.
  • Epilepsy-Protocol High-Resolution MRI (3T): Advanced thin-slice MRI techniques tailored to identify subtle structural abnormalities such as hippocampal sclerosis, focal cortical dysplasias, vascular malformations, or micro-lesions.
  • Functional & Metabolic Neuroimaging (PET / SPECT / fMRI): Positron Emission Tomography (PET) and ictal SPECT scans evaluate brain glucose metabolism and blood flow during and between seizures to localize the seizure focus. Functional MRI (fMRI) maps speech and memory areas relative to the seizure zone.
  • Stereo-EEG (sEEG) / Invasive Monitoring: In complex cases, fine depth electrodes are placed directly into target brain areas through keyhole openings to precisely record internal electrical signals before planning curative surgery.

How is it Treated?

Management ranges from targeted medical therapy to modern functional neurosurgical interventions designed for drug-resistant epilepsy:

  • Anti-Seizure Medications (ASMs): First-line medical therapy tailored to the specific seizure type, effectively controlling seizures in approximately 70% of individuals.
  • Resective Epilepsy Surgery (e.g., Anterior Temporal Lobectomy / Lesionectomy): For medically refractory epilepsy (when medications fail), high-precision microsurgery to remove the exact scar or structural focal point causing seizures while preserving surrounding speech, motor, and memory functions.
  • Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS): A functional neuromodulation device implanted under the collarbone that sends mild, regular electrical impulses up the vagus nerve to the brain to disrupt abnormal seizure networks.
  • Responsive Neurostimulation (RNS System) & Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS): Advanced closed-loop neural implants that constantly monitor brain waves, detect abnormal electrical patterns at their onset, and immediately deliver imperceptible electrical stimulation to stop seizures before symptoms start.
  • Disconnective Surgery & Laser Interstitial Thermal Therapy (LITT): Keyhole or minimally invasive procedures—such as MRI-guided laser ablation—used to heat and neutralize deep seizure targets or sever nerve tracts (e.g., corpus callosotomy) to prevent seizure spread.
