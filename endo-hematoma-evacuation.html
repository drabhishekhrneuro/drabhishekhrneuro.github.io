Endoscopic Brain Hematoma Evacuation

What Is Endoscopic Brain Hematoma Evacuation?

Imagine a sudden stroke or head injury causing bleeding directly inside the delicate tissue of the brain (intracerebral hemorrhage) or within its fluid spaces. When blood pools into a confined space, it forms a clot (hematoma) that creates dangerous mass effect, squashing surrounding brain cells and raising pressure inside the rigid skull.

Endoscopic brain hematoma evacuation is a modern, minimally invasive surgical technique that uses a slender, high-definition camera (endoscope) and specialized working channels to remove deep-seated blood clots safely through a tiny keyhole opening.

Think of this procedure as precise keyhole clearance of trapped blood within a delicate environment:

  • Minimally Invasive Access: Instead of large traditional open brain incisions and wide brain retraction, the neurosurgeon creates a small, coin-sized opening in the skull (burr hole or mini-craniotomy) to reach the clot directly.
  • Direct Endoscopic Visualization: Working under magnified, high-definition illumination inside the fluid or clot cavity, the surgeon can see around corners and meticulously aspirate and wash out clotted blood while protecting nearby blood vessels and healthy brain fibers.
  • Immediate Pressure Relief: The primary goal is to rapidly decompress the brain, stop ongoing bleeding sources, minimize secondary injury from toxic breakdown products of blood, and improve neurological recovery.

When Is This Procedure Recommended?

Endoscopic evacuation is indicated when an acute or subacute brain hemorrhage causes severe mass effect, neurological decline, or life-threatening pressure:

Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage (Stroke)

Large blood clots resulting from high blood pressure, aneurysmal rupture, or vascular malformations located deep within the brain lobes, basal ganglia, or cerebellum.

Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH)

When blood fills the brain's internal fluid chambers (ventricles), causing obstructive hydrocephalus and acute pressure spikes requiring endoscopic washout and drainage.

Progressive Neurological Deterioration

When a patient experiences declining alertness, worsening weakness, or brainstem compression despite medical management.

Refractory Intracranial Pressure

Cases where rising pressure inside the skull fails to respond to medications, requiring prompt surgical clot removal to prevent permanent brain injury or herniation.

How Is the Procedure Planned & Prepared?

Emergency or urgent preoperative assessment focuses on mapping the exact location of the bleed and stabilizing systemic health:

  • Emergency Non-Contrast Brain CT & CT Angiography (CTA): Rapid imaging scans that pinpoint the precise 3D volume, location, and boundaries of the hematoma, while ruling out underlying vascular malformations or aneurysms as the cause of bleeding.
  • Coagulation & Blood Profile Screening: Immediate laboratory testing to check clotting function, platelet counts, and reversal of any blood-thinning medications (anticoagulants) prior to surgery.
  • Computer-Assisted Trajectory Planning: Integration of CT scan data into neuronavigation software to calculate the safest, straightest keyhole path through the brain tissue to the center of the clot.
  • Anesthetic Stabilization: Securing airway safety and managing blood pressure parameters under general anesthesia to optimize cerebral perfusion.

What Happens During the Surgery?

The procedure is executed through structured microsurgical and endoscopic steps designed for maximal clot clearance with minimal trauma:

  • Stage 1: Keyhole Skull Access: Under general anesthesia, a small incision is made in the scalp, and a burr hole or mini-craniotomy (roughly the size of a coin) is opened directly over the planned surgical trajectory.
  • Stage 2: Endoscopic Insertion into the Clot: Guided by neuronavigation, a transparent sheath with a high-definition neuroendoscope is advanced directly into the hematoma cavity.
  • Stage 3: Clot Liquefaction & Evacuation: Utilizing specialized micro-suction instruments, delicate forceps, and continuous warm saline irrigation, the solid and semi-clotted blood is systematically broken down and gently washed out.
  • Stage 4: Hemostasis & Inspection: Once the cavity is clear of blood clots, the surgeon inspects the walls of the cavity under direct endoscopic vision to identify and stop any active bleeding points using micro-coagulation.
  • Stage 5: Drainage Placement & Closure: A temporary drainage catheter may be left in the cavity or ventricles to monitor post-op fluid. The bone opening is secured, and the scalp is closed with sutures.

Post-Operative Recovery & Rehabilitation

Recovery following endoscopic hematoma evacuation centers on intensive neuro-monitoring, blood pressure control, and structured rehabilitation:

  • Neuro-ICU Care (First 24–72 Hours): Patients are managed in an intensive care unit for continuous blood pressure regulation, intracranial pressure monitoring, and neurological assessments.
  • Follow-Up Imaging: A repeat brain CT scan is performed within 24 hours of surgery to confirm successful removal of the bulk of the hematoma and check for any residual bleeding or swelling.
  • Blood Pressure & Medical Management: Strict control of systemic blood pressure is maintained to protect fragile blood vessels and prevent re-bleeding.
  • Comprehensive Rehabilitation: Physical, occupational, and speech therapy begin as soon as the patient is stable to retrain lost motor control, balance, swallowing, and speech functions.
