Whiplash Injury

What Is a Whiplash Injury?

A whiplash injury happens when the neck is suddenly and forcefully whipped back and forth—much like the cracking of a whip. It is most common in rear-end car accidents, but it can also happen during sports contact, falls or amusement park rides.

Think of your neck as a flexible column supported by a combination of rubber bands (ligaments) and cables (muscles) that hold your head upright:

  • The Sudden Snap: When an unexpected impact hits, your body gets pushed forward, but your heavy head stays behind for a split second, snapping backward (hyperextension).
  • The Rebound: Immediately after, your head snaps violently forward (hyperflexion).
  • The Damage: This rapid, double-direction movement overstretches or causes tiny micro-tears in those supporting muscles, ligaments and nerves of spinal cord.

Key Symptoms & What to Look For

Symptoms don't always start right away; it often takes 24 to 48 hours for inflammation to build up following the injury:

Heavy or Stiff Neck

Turning your head feels like trying to move a rusty hinge or wearing an invisible, tight neck collar.

Base-of-Skull Headaches

Pain that starts at the top of the neck and creeps up into the back of the head.

Shoulder & Upper Back Aches

Feeling like you've carried a heavy backpack all day, caused by nearby muscles overworking to stabilize your neck.

Pins and Needles

Tingling sensations down into the arms or hands if a nerve root gets irritated during the whip movement.

How Is It Treated & Managed?

Most people recover fully within a few weeks using conservative, supportive care:

  • Gentle Movement (Not Prolonged Rest): Resting for a day or two helps, but keeping the neck completely still for too long can make stiffness worse. Gentle, painless motion keeps blood flowing to help muscles heal.
  • Ice and Heat Therapy: Applying ice packs during the first 48 hours helps reduce localized swelling, followed by warm compresses to relax tight, spastic muscles.
  • Physiotherapy: Targeted stretches and strengthening exercises help restore your neck's natural range of motion and overall flexibility.
